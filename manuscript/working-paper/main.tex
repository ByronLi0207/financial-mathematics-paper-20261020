{\LARGE\bfseries Historical Response Selection for Sampled FX Net-Position Peaks}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\large A fixed comparison and an exploratory state-retrieval extension}\par\vspace{6pt}
Working paper - 21 October 2026
\end{center}
\begin{abstract}
Using one foreign-exchange market maker's sampled aggregate client-flow positions from 2014–2017, we compare empirical quantile forecasts for the maximum absolute source-reported USD net position over the current and next H records. We vary historical episode selection and response representation. An original fixed comparison is extended by signed-state nearest-neighbor retrieval after the evaluation interval had been inspected. The primary comparisons use 6,625 common issue origins, a .95 quantile preference and fully completed labels; the extension selects 360 or 180 episodes from a rolling pool of 720. At H=6, historical headrooms have 2.08\% lower mean pinball loss than raw current-anchor scenarios, with more breaches and larger overruns; the ranking reverses at H=24. In the extension's fixed primary comparison, nearest360 retrieval reduces floored native-peak-quantile loss by 31.83\% relative to recent360 retrieval, but remains worse than the original full-window headroom and scenario rules. All 12 matched retrieval loss contrasts are negative, while every matched scenario contrast has more breaches and greater mean positive overrun. Elementary common-set bounds explain how nearby starting positions can make representations agree without certifying accuracy. The findings identify finite-record forecast tradeoffs, with exploratory retrieval evidence and no population superiority inference. The recorded proxy and passive forecasts do not establish conditional coverage, independent external validity or policy, profit and funding effects.
\end{abstract}
\section*{1. Introduction}
A sampled net position can reach a large magnitude and finish near its starting level. We study the largest absolute source-reported USD net position at issue and over the next H records, using sampled aggregate client flow from one foreign-exchange market maker. A forecast level is breached when at least one of those recorded positions has a greater absolute value. H counts source records, including those separated by 49-hour gaps. The object is recorded net-position scale, rather than a complete hedged book or a funding requirement.

For this passive forecasting task, we ask how replacing recent completed episodes with equally many episodes whose signed starting positions are closest to the current position changes realized losses and the balance between overruns and unused allowance. We compare native peaks (K), headrooms above each episode's starting magnitude (F), and signed increments reanchored at the current position (S), under a stipulated .95 quantile preference. Signed distance distinguishes positive and negative starts of equal magnitude; matching magnitude alone need not make native and reanchored peak reports agree.

The original fixed comparison evaluates headrooms, reanchored increment envelopes and three simple references on 6,625 common issue origins. The extension was designed after those results had been inspected; its complete menu and primary contrast were recorded before the added forecasts were calculated. It crosses three representations with five historical sets. The primary contrast compares floored native-peak quantiles from the 360 nearest signed starting positions and the 360 most recent origins, selected from the same 720 fully completed episodes, at a six-record horizon. A second fixed count and a 24-record horizon provide sensitivity evidence. These remain exploratory comparisons on the inspected interval.

Common-set bounds connect differences between the three reports to historical starting-position distances. Exact signed-anchor matches make all three agree in real arithmetic. Nearest selection minimizes the largest selected signed distance among equal-count subsets; changes in actual report gaps and loss remain empirical questions. We report loss alongside issued levels, breaches, overrun, unused allowance, selected age and anchor distances. Construction agreement itself supplies no accuracy guarantee.

In the original primary comparison, F lowers loss by 2.08\% relative to S, with more breaches and larger overruns; the ranking reverses at H=24. The extension lowers primary K loss by 31.83\% relative to equal-count recent retrieval, a weak baseline, while leaving the original full-window leaders ahead. Every matched S retrieval gain accompanies more breaches and greater mean positive overrun. The loss gains therefore have distinct breach and severity consequences across representations.

\section*{2. Closest work and mature method transfer}
The closest empirical lineage is Wisniewski, Lindsay and Lindsay (2020), who already use nearest-neighbor and other regression engines on this source, with lagged positions and calendar or trading-session features, to forecast next-step signed-position intervals. Their comparisons include quantile, conformal and normalized-conformal variants; reported pinball scores average the two endpoint-quantile losses. Chapter 6 of Wisniewski's thesis continues same-source endpoint prediction with normalized and regularized constructions (Wisniewski, 2024). We instead evaluate an absolute multi-record maximum including the issue record under a single upper-quantile loss. Identical marginal distributions at each future record can yield different peak quantiles when their dependence changes. Published endpoint numbers therefore cannot benchmark this response. Suitable quantile engines could be refitted to completed peak or headroom labels; that same-target, richer-feature comparison remains unrun here.

Financial path extrema are established forecasting and risk objects. MaxVaR studies the worst mark-to-market loss relative to initial wealth over a monitoring horizon (Boudoukh, Richardson, Stanton and Whitelaw, 2004). Maximum cumulative payment-cash deficits (van den Hoogen, 2017) and maximum collateral-adjusted contingent exposure with marking decisions (Liao and Theodosopoulos, 2005) address other peak-related financial questions. The present application differs in its sampled two-sided net-position response, state and units, rather than originating financial peak forecasting.

The transferred operations are also mature. Hull and White (1998) update historical shocks using predicted volatility; Barone-Adesi, Giannopoulos and Vosper (1999) resample standardized innovation vectors to generate recursive conditional paths before revaluing derivative portfolios. S instead translates raw observed net-position increments, without a fitted volatility filter or dynamics model. Biau and Patra (2011) aggregate truncated nearest-neighbor quantile experts using growing samples. Their stated long-run average-loss guarantee assumes stationary and ergodic data with moment and distributional regularity. Our fixed-window, one-state rule with delayed overlapping labels defines a different procedure; it inherits neither that consistency result nor coverage.

Two mature conditional routes provide further comparators. CAViaR models a time-varying conditional quantile through an autoregressive specification estimated by regression quantiles (Engle and Manganelli, 2004). SPCI predicts conditional residual quantiles from residual histories; its analyzed quantile-random-forest construction has an asymptotic conditional interval-coverage result under specified conditions (Xu and Xie, 2023). Transferring either route would require a declared single upper peak or headroom quantile, issue-known features and residuals whose H-record labels have completed. The possible zero-headroom atom, overlapping labels and source gaps require checking the chosen model and guarantee assumptions. Neither method is fitted here, and their published empirical results do not benchmark this response. They identify unrun same-target competitors, without validating the present fixed-pool retrieval rule.

The contribution is a bounded application comparison: a multi-record peak response, completed-label timing, signed single-anchor retrieval crossed with response representation, and elementary common-set bounds explaining when those representations approach one another. It introduces neither nearest neighbors to this source nor a new estimator, independent dealer record or comparison against the strongest conditional models.

\section*{3. Data, recorded response and completed information}
The input is the one-hour member of version 2 of the Net Position Time Series Dataset, with 18,576 records from 5 February 2014 01:00 through 24 January 2017 00:00. Wisniewski et al. (2020) describe sampled aggregate client-flow positions across five liquid FX pairs. Only datetime and NetPosUsd are used. A netted USD scalar does not reconstruct currency-level gross positions, hedge transactions, collateral or cash movements. The source's timezone and historical publication/vintage convention are unverified.

There are 18,420 one-hour and 155 forty-nine-hour adjacent gaps. We retain the file's chronology without sorting, deleting paths across gaps or interpolating missing intervals. Consequently H counts subsequent records, rather than elapsed hours. Using zero-based observation indices, let N\textsubscript{t} be the signed source-reported position, observed before issue. Define

\[
B_t=|N_t|,\qquad P_{t,H}=\max_{0\le h\le H}|N_{t+h}|,\qquad G_{t,H}=P_{t,H}-B_t.
\]
Including h=0 gives the known floor B\textsubscript{t} and nonnegative headroom G. The peak includes an interior recorded spike even if the final position returns to its starting value. It does not bound an unobserved excursion between records. The retrospective issue convention treats N\textsubscript{t} as available immediately before issue, rather than establishing historical live-publication availability.

At issue t the extension's eligible pool is I\textsubscript{t}=\{t\(-\)H\(-\)719,...,t\(-\)H\}. Each of its 720 historical origins i has a response ending at i+H\(\le\)t. The latest eligible response window ends at t and includes the already observed issue record. Earlier evaluation outcomes enter later pools only after completion. Adjacent peaks and rolling pools overlap; completed-label eligibility does not make them independent.

The 720 response-end indices i+H range from t\(-\)719 through t at both horizons, although their starting origins differ. For example, at the first issue t=11927, candidate origins are 11202–11921 for H=6 and 11184–11903 for H=24; both sets of historical responses end at indices 11208–11927. The new response ends at 11933 or 11951, respectively. These indices illustrate the fixed timing rule, not additional data or forecast results.

For each eligible origin retain its native peak P\textsubscript{i,H}, headroom G\textsubscript{i,H} and directional increment extrema

\[
\begin{aligned}a_{i,H}&=\min_{0\le h\le H}(N_{i+h}-N_i),\\b_{i,H}&=\max_{0\le h\le H}(N_{i+h}-N_i),\qquad a_{i,H}\le0\le b_{i,H}.\end{aligned}
\]
These extrema remain paired by episode and determine the absolute peak after raw translation of the sampled increment path. They are not merely its two endpoints. Forming each translated absolute peak before the quantile operation generally differs from taking separate directional quantiles. This deterministic sufficiency does not specify the next path's conditional law.

\section*{4. Historical selection and response representation}
The extension uses five fixed sets at each issue. The reduced counts, 360 and 180, retain half and a quarter of the candidate pool and were fixed before the extension's losses were calculated. Recent sets take the last m eligible origins. Nearest sets take the m smallest signed-state distances |N\textsubscript{i}\(-\)N\textsubscript{t}|, preferring the most recent origin when distances tie. Historical response values and future query outcomes never resolve distance ties. Selected paired rows retain chronological order.

For a selected set J of m rows, Q\textsubscript{k} denotes the kth unweighted order statistic with duplicates retained and no interpolation or jitter. We use the available ranks in Table 1. Appendix C gives their empirical quantile interpretation; the rank convention does not establish coverage for the dependent historical pool.

The three response representations issue the following levels, with H suppressed on historical labels:

\[
F_J=B_t+Q_k\{P_i-|N_i|:i\in J\}.
\]
\[
S_J=B_t+Q_k\{\max(N_t+b_i,-N_t-a_i)-B_t:i\in J\}.
\]
\[
K_J=\max\{B_t,Q_k\{P_i:i\in J\}\}.
\]
F retains headroom relative to historical starting magnitude; S translates signed increments to the current position; K retains native peaks subject to the current floor. K is the method labeled L in the original artifacts. The files call an issued forecast level C its 'capacity'; throughout the paper this is a passive reported level. All three already use current information: F/K use B\textsubscript{t}, and S also uses signed N\textsubscript{t}. Retrieval changes historical membership. Equal-count recent and nearest sets may differ in age, scale, regimes and dependence.

F has the algebraic form of an upper residual-quantile endpoint around the fixed baseline f(N)=|N|. Because P\textsubscript{i,H}\(\ge\)|N\textsubscript{i}|, its residual G\textsubscript{i,H} is also the absolute prediction error of that baseline. This identity does not implement the fitted, split-calibration and symmetric-interval procedure of Wisniewski et al. (2020), nor establish conformal validity for the overlapping archive.

The original five-rule comparison also includes the radius reference R=B\textsubscript{t}+Q\textsubscript{k}\{max(b\textsubscript{i},\(-\)a\textsubscript{i})\} and the known-floor reference P0=B\textsubscript{t}, on its full n-row pool. The extension implements only F/S/K. Appendix C specifies the stored arithmetic and operation order.

An elementary construction bound explains what matching can control. For one common nonempty set J and the same valid rank, put r\textsubscript{abs}=max\textsubscript{i in J}||N\textsubscript{i}|\(-\)B\textsubscript{t}| and r\textsubscript{signed}=max\textsubscript{i in J}|N\textsubscript{i}\(-\)N\textsubscript{t}|. In real arithmetic,

\[
\begin{aligned}|F_J-K_J|&\le r_{\rm abs},\qquad |S_J-K_J|\le r_{\rm signed},\\|F_J-S_J|&\le r_{\rm abs}+r_{\rm signed}\le2r_{\rm signed}.\end{aligned}
\]
Appendix A proves the bounds using floor projection and order-statistic stability. If every selected signed anchor equals the query, all three reports agree; if only every selected magnitude equals the query magnitude, F=K while S can differ. Plateaus can also give equal F/S reports at distant anchors. Nearest retrieval minimizes the largest selected signed distance among equally sized subsets, but need not minimize actual report differences or loss. These common-set bounds do not compare forecasts built from different selected histories, and they contain no statement about future calibration.

\section*{5. Design histories, score and reporting}
The original specification was recorded at 05:46:37 UTC on 21 October 2026 and publicly committed before its target-specific forecasts and losses were calculated. It fixes H=6, n=720, \(\tau\)=19/20 as primary, with six one-factor changes: H=12 or 24, n=480 or 1440, and \(\tau\)=.90 or .99. The five rules and seven settings yield 35 original summary rows. This is a retrospective specification exercise with prior source-literature knowledge, not external preregistration or prospective collection.

The extension was conceived after those results had been inspected. Its human-readable protocol and hash-pinned machine specification were publicly recorded in commits 63703b9 and 69d5bb6 before the added forecasts. They fix H=6 and 24, the five sets in Table 1 and F/S/K: 15 configurations per horizon, 30 in total. The six full720 F/S/K rows repeat original F/S/L references. Recording the extension before execution limits further discretion but cannot turn the old interval into an unseen holdout.

Both designs use the same 6,625 issue origins: zero-based observation indices 11927 through 18551 inclusive, from naive source timestamp 1 January 2016 00:00 through 23 January 2017 00:00. The common original full-menu rule retains the future records required by H=24. Earlier evaluation labels become usable later only when completed. Changing H alters both the future peak and the eligible historical origin indices, so cross-horizon comparisons do not isolate the maximum operator alone.

For stored error e=P\(-\)C, pinball loss is

\[
\rho_\tau(e)=\tau\max(e,0)+(1-\tau)\max(-e,0),\qquad \tau=19/20\ \text{in the extension}.
\]
The underprediction weight is nineteen times the overprediction weight. The score retains the source's USD units and expresses a stipulated forecast preference, rather than an estimated funding or shortage cost. With a finite first absolute moment, expected pinball loss is minimized by its possibly nonunique \(\tau\)-quantiles, allowing atoms. Other strictly consistent scores can target the same \(\tau\) while valuing errors differently (Gneiting, 2011). We report rankings under the fixed pinball score. Scoring consistency does not make a historical report conditionally correct. Original rows with different \(\tau\) express different objectives and are not pooled into an across-preference winner.

Reported means and paired differences are equal-weight averages over the 6,625 listed origins. The original primary contrast is mean loss(F) minus mean loss(S) in its primary setting. The extension's separate primary contrast is mean loss(nearest360 K) minus mean loss(recent360 K), at H=6; a nonnegative mean would fail its finite-record improvement criterion. H=24 repeats retrieval in a context adverse to the original F/S comparison, and m=180 is fixed sensitivity. Neither may replace an unfavorable primary contrast. All 12 nearest-minus-recent and 10 same-set F-minus-S contrasts are reported regardless of sign.

For an issued level C\(\ge\)B, extra allowance is C\(-\)B. Positive overrun and unused allowance give the score decomposition

\[
O=(P-C)^+,\qquad U=(C-P)^+,\qquad \rho_\tau(P-C)=\tau O+(1-\tau)U,\quad z^+=\max(z,0).
\]
Every configuration reports mean loss, level and extra allowance; strict breach count/rate; mean O; conditional overrun with its own breach denominator; and mean U. Strict breach is P\( > \)C, with no tolerance changing its sign. Conditional overrun averages O only over that rule's breach events; zero breaches make it undefined. The target can have an atom at B, so even a true quantile's strict exceedance need not equal 1\(-\)\(\tau\). Nominal breach proximity is not a calibration certificate.

Geometry reports both anchor radii, mean selected age in records, the mean absolute F/S gap and exact equal-capacity counts. Primary monthly retrieval contrasts retain all 13 inclusive calendar months at both H; groups below 30 origins retain counts/status while suppressing metrics. These are descriptive groups, not independent replications or an adaptive strategy. No new population test, confidence interval or bootstrap is attached to the extension. Original block-resampling profiles concern only the original full720 F/S contrast; Appendix B preserves their descriptive status.

\section*{8. Interpretation and limits}
Nearest-state retrieval lowers loss relative to equal-count recent retrieval throughout the fixed menu, with the largest absolute gains for K. The tradeoffs differ by representation. All four matched F comparisons have fewer breaches and lower mean positive overrun, but higher conditional mean overrun. The primary K comparison also has fewer breaches and lower mean positive overrun, but higher conditional mean overrun. Every matched S comparison has more breaches and greater mean positive overrun. Conditional means use each method's own breach set. The original full-window leaders remain ahead, and the one-state rule is not benchmarked against the richer information in Wisniewski et al. (2020), Wisniewski (2024), conditional regressions or fitted filtered historical simulation.

Smaller representation gaps accord with the finite geometry, without identifying an accurate conditional forecast. Raw translation alone does not establish that historical envelopes represent the future conditional envelope law. Appendix A's known-linear-dynamics illustration gives a horizon-dependent anchor correction for innovation replay, whereas raw translation retains the full anchor difference at every step. This deterministic illustration fits no coefficient and establishes no inventory-control mechanism for the source.

In Avellaneda and Stoikov's (2008) dealer model, quote offsets affect fill intensities and future share inventory. Using a forecast to change quotes could also change the process being forecast. The policies behind this aggregate USD record are unobserved, so the passive comparison does not evaluate such an intervention. The stipulated 19:1 score weights allow less unused allowance to offset greater overrun; funding prices and shortage costs are unobserved. Even accurate prediction of this proxy would not establish cash adequacy, gross exposure, P\&L or the effects of changing quotes, hedges or risk limits.

The evidence comes from one aggregate 2014–2017 record. Reexecution, an identical-hash download and a same-source thesis support reproducibility, not transfer across dealers or regimes. Independent inventory data require an audit of aggregation, units, sampling and release timing; price returns and weekly position reports measure different objects.

Overlapping maxima, shared histories and persistent positions create dependence. The reported averages summarize the listed origins; they do not establish expected performance on another record. No argument establishes that the selected historical responses estimate the future peak's conditional law given the signed state, or that this state captures all relevant issue-time information. The fixed-pool rule differs from the growing-sample strategy behind the cited nearest-neighbor guarantee. Selected row counts are not independent tail-observation counts; geometry, empirical ranks and breach frequencies do not establish coverage. The original resampling has its own unverified inferential premises (Appendix B) and supplies no uncertainty for the extension. The extension remains exploratory because its interval had already been inspected.

\section*{9. Conclusion}
Historical selection and response representation produce distinct forecast tradeoffs for sampled client-flow net-position peaks. Original headroom pooling lowers primary loss by 2.08\% relative to raw scenario reanchoring, with more breaches and greater overrun; the ranking reverses at H=24. Exploratory state retrieval improves equal-count recent rules, including a 31.83\% primary reduction for the weak native-peak baseline, while the original full-window leaders remain ahead. \section*{Appendix A. Finite geometry and its predictive limits}
\subsection*{A.1. Common-set projection bounds}
These are elementary finite-array arguments, rather than new asymptotic or coverage results. Fix a nonempty common selected set J, an available rank 1\(\le\)k\(\le\)|J|, query x=N\textsubscript{t}, floor B=|x| and completed historical windows. All identities in this appendix concern real arithmetic. Write B\textsubscript{i}=|N\textsubscript{i}|, d\textsubscript{i,h}=N\textsubscript{i+h}\(-\)N\textsubscript{i}, with d\textsubscript{i,0}=0, and define

\[
A_i=B+P_i-B_i,\qquad T_i=\max_{0\le h\le H}|x+d_{i,h}|,\qquad C_B(z)=\max(B,z).
\]
Both A\textsubscript{i} and T\textsubscript{i} are at least B. The projection C\textsubscript{B} is nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. It commutes with a finite order statistic, as does a common translation. Hence F\textsubscript{J}=Q\textsubscript{k}(A), S\textsubscript{J}=Q\textsubscript{k}(T) and K\textsubscript{J}=Q\textsubscript{k}(C\textsubscript{B}(P)). Since C\textsubscript{B}(A\textsubscript{i})=A\textsubscript{i},

\[
|A_i-C_B(P_i)|\le |A_i-P_i|=|B-B_i|.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality bounds each path value's change under a starting-position translation by |x\(-\)N\textsubscript{i}|. Taking a maximum gives |T\textsubscript{i}\(-\)P\textsubscript{i}|\(\le\)|x\(-\)N\textsubscript{i}|. Applying the same floor projection preserves that bound because T\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)B. Finally, paired order statistics are nonexpansive in the maximum coordinate difference: if |u\textsubscript{i}\(-\)v\textsubscript{i}|\(\le\)r for every i, then u\textsubscript{i}\(\le\)v\textsubscript{i}+r and conversely; monotonicity and common translation give |Q\textsubscript{k}(u)\(-\)Q\textsubscript{k}(v)|\(\le\)r. These observations prove the two K bounds in Section 4. The triangle inequality and ||N\textsubscript{i}|\(-\)|x||\(\le\)|N\textsubscript{i}\(-\)x| prove the stated F/S bound. Keeping the rank fixed while changing one side's historical rows does not by itself justify these common-set comparison bounds.

Among all size-m subsets, selecting the m smallest signed distances minimizes the largest selected distance: any alternative must include an element at least as far away as the mth ordered distance. This finite minimax fact, with the declared tie convention, concerns the signed radius. It does not minimize the absolute-anchor radius, the sum of the two radii, the actual report gap or a forecast loss. For every fixed realized y, pinball loss is max(\(\tau\),1\(-\)\(\tau\))-Lipschitz in its report, so a report-gap bound also bounds the absolute loss difference. Such stability supplies no sign for the realized or expected loss difference.

\subsection*{A.2. Envelope plateaus and the role of signed anchors}
Put u\textsubscript{i}=b\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)0 and v\textsubscript{i}=\(-\)a\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)0. The headroom of an increment envelope at any anchor z is

\[
g_i(z)=\max(z+u_i,-z+v_i)-|z|=\begin{cases}\max(u_i,v_i-2z),&z\ge0,\\\max(u_i+2z,v_i),&z\le0.\end{cases}
\]
The branches agree at zero. Each is 2-Lipschitz on its half-line, so the whole function is 2-Lipschitz; its range lies in [min(u\textsubscript{i},v\textsubscript{i}),max(u\textsubscript{i},v\textsubscript{i})]. Applying these two bounds to g\textsubscript{i}(N\textsubscript{i}) and g\textsubscript{i}(x), and then order-statistic stability, gives

\[
|F_J-S_J|\le\max_{i\in J}\min\{2|x-N_i|,|u_i-v_i|\}.
\]
For example, u\textsubscript{i}=v\textsubscript{i} in every row makes F\textsubscript{J}=S\textsubscript{J} at arbitrary anchors. Nonnegative query/source anchors together with u\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)v\textsubscript{i} in every row also suffice; the nonpositive analogue uses v\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)u\textsubscript{i}. These are sufficient conditions, not a characterization: pooled order statistics can coincide even when paired rows differ. The range bound also gives F\textsubscript{J},S\textsubscript{J}\(\le\)R\textsubscript{J}, where R\textsubscript{J}=B+Q\textsubscript{k}\{max(u\textsubscript{i},v\textsubscript{i}):i in J\}. This is a theoretical same-set ordering; no R retrieval configuration is evaluated in the extension, and the ordering does not make R\textsubscript{J} preferable under a given loss.

If every B\textsubscript{i}\(\le\)B, then A\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)C\textsubscript{B}(P\textsubscript{i}) and F\textsubscript{J}\(\ge\)K\textsubscript{J}. If every B\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)B, then C\textsubscript{B}(P\textsubscript{i})=P\textsubscript{i}\(\ge\)A\textsubscript{i} and F\textsubscript{J}\(\le\)K\textsubscript{J}. Equal absolute anchors force F=K but leave signs relevant to S. A coherent full-pool example uses c\( > \)0, H=1, n=720, \(\tau\)=19/20, t=721 and N\textsubscript{s}=(\(-\)1)\textsuperscript{s+1}c for 0\(\le\)s\(\le\)721. The mature pool is J=\{1,...,720\}, with 360 anchors of each sign and current N\textsubscript{t}=c. All native peaks equal c and headrooms equal zero, so F=K=c. Raw reanchoring gives 360 peaks c and 360 peaks 3c; rank 685 gives S=3c. Thus r\textsubscript{abs}=0 while r\textsubscript{signed}=2c and |S\(-\)K|=2c. This artificial path respects maturity and an available corrected rank; it is not an empirical claim or a claim about which rows nearest retrieval selects.

\subsection*{A.4. Raw translation versus replay under known dynamics}
For interpretation only, suppose N\textsubscript{s+1}=\(\mu\)+\(\phi\)(N\textsubscript{s}\(-\)\(\mu\))+\(\epsilon\)\textsubscript{s+1}, with a common known center and coefficient. Replay a historical window's ordered innovations from query x. Subtracting the two recursions and using induction gives

\[
M_{i,h}(x)=N_{i+h}+\phi^h(x-N_i),\qquad W_{i,h}(x)=N_{i+h}+(x-N_i).
\]
Taking the maximum absolute path value yields

\[
\left|\max_h|W_{i,h}(x)|-\max_h|M_{i,h}(x)|\right|\le\max_{0\le h\le H}|1-\phi^h|\,|x-N_i|.
\]
For 0\(\le\)\(\phi\)\(\le\)1 the coefficient is 1\(-\)\(\phi\)\textsuperscript{H}; at \(\phi\)=1 the paths coincide. The identity needs no innovation law, while treating past innovations as representative of future conditional innovations is a separate assumption. Neither the recursion nor that assumption is fitted or validated here. The extrema suffice to evaluate the peak after raw translation, but generally not after the dynamic correction, which depends on path order. Conditional validity of historical envelopes remains a separate question.

\section*{Appendix C. Rank, arithmetic and replication conventions}
For m sorted responses z\textsubscript{(1)}\(\le\)...\(\le\)z\textsubscript{(m)}, the corrected rank is k=ceil((m+1)\(\tau\)). If k\( > \)m, this implementation stops rather than inserting an infinite report or changing the rank. All declared cells have an available rank. The original seven ranks are 685,685,685,457,1369,649,714 in the Table B1 order. In the extension \(\tau\)=19/20 and m in \{720,360,180\}, so m\(\tau\) in \{684,342,171\} is an integer. The empirical pinball-minimizer interval is [z\textsubscript{(m\(\tau\))},z\textsubscript{(m\(\tau\)+1)}], including ties; the configured corrected rank chooses its upper endpoint. This particular relationship is not asserted for the original \(\tau\)=.99 cell, nor does any empirical-minimizer identity establish future coverage.

All forecasts use stored float64 values, duplicate-preserving order statistics and the original operation order. For S, the translated peak is computed first, B is subtracted before ranking, and B is added afterward. Replacing this with a mathematically equivalent differently rounded expression can alter stored capacity ties, strict flags and scores. The scientific tables use direct error P\(-\)C; the paired summary uses compensated aggregation of per-origin loss differences, rather than subtracting displayed or independently rounded global means. Empty CSV values and JSON nulls have documented undefined reasons. Finite numerical reconciliation tolerances are kept separate from event definitions.

The extension's per-origin geometry tolerance is 64\(\epsilon\) times the maximum of one, current floor, historical anchor magnitudes, native peaks, all three reports and the two anchor radii. The input source, specification and code identities are checked before accepting a run and rechecked after computation.
