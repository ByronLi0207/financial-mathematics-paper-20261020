Inventory monitoring concerns the largest position reached during a horizon as well as its endpoint. We ask when conditional quantile methods improve forecasts of this peak over simple historical rules. Using an hourly FX client-flow position record and weekly Treasury-bill position aggregates, we compare established methods on matched completed-label histories and update schedules. Quantile regression reduces six-step FX pinball loss by 12.7\%, with a descriptive Diebold--Mariano p-value below 0.001. The original Treasury specification increases loss by 50.8\%. An exploratory shared penalty menu largely removes that deterioration while preserving the FX gain. Score gains coexist with more breaches, and the main comparisons have crossing rankings across consistent quantile scores. The contributions are a target-specific benchmark, an ordered specification comparison, and joint score-versus-breach evidence. The evaluation is retrospective: the FX period had previously been inspected, and Treasury observations are replayed by economic date using a downloaded vintage.
