Inventory monitoring concerns the largest position reached during a horizon as well as its endpoint. Using an hourly FX client-flow position record and weekly Treasury-bill aggregates, we compare historical rules and conditional quantiles on matched completed-label histories and update schedules. Long-history regression reduces six-step FX pinball loss by \FXPrimaryGain{}\%, while its original Treasury counterpart increases loss by \TreasuryOriginalExcess{}\%. Expanding the Treasury penalty menu reduces that excess to \TreasuryExpandedExcess{}\%, approaching an intercept-only empirical-quantile reference while preserving the FX gain. Across \PanelSeriesCount{} additional archived weekly series, expansion reduces median excess loss from \PanelOriginalMedian{}\% to \PanelExpandedMedian{}\%, but regression still loses in \PanelExpandedLosses{} of \PanelCellCount{} cells. Restrictive tuning can therefore explain substantial deterioration without explaining it all. Lower mean score coexists with more breaches, and neither primary comparison dominates across all consistent quantile scores. The analyses are retrospective: FX outcomes were previously inspected, Treasury is replayed by economic date from a downloaded vintage, and the panel shares dealer reporting and overlapping positions.
