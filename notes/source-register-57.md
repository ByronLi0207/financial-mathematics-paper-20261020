## Science synthesis — 114 words

<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_START -->
Giacomini and White compare forecasting methods with bounded estimation memory as evaluation grows; ordinary expanding windows are excluded. Conditional equality uses issue-time information; predictable instruments test selected implications. Unconditional equality requires long-run covariance. Instrument-weighted loss differences need mixing, sufficient moments and nondegenerate covariance; score consistency alone does not establish these. Multistep covariance cutoffs follow the full conditional null, not independence. Simulations distinguish method-level from model-level nulls; rejection rates under different nulls are not interchangeable size estimates. They show limitations of zero-lag unconditional variance under serial dependence. Retrospective fitted selection differs from the rolling 200-loss hybrid. The macroeconomic application offers no universal selector guarantee. These are asymptotic results, not anytime validity; results were not reproduced.
