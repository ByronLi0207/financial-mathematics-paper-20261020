# Literature update 03 — nearby applications and limits of transfer

20 October 2026. The project remains in literature review. These are original reading notes, not empirical results, a selected topic, or a claim that the requested reading duration has been completed. “Selected full text” means the listed sections were read; it does not mean every page or proof was audited.

| Primary source and fixed version | Actual reading scope | Consequence for possible transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Jing Li and Haibin Zhu, [Sequential Confidence Sets for Coverage-Constrained Conformal Model Selection, 2609.28522v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28522v1), 22 September 2026 | Selected full text: §§3–5, B.1, B.5, G.1–G.2 | Coverage-constrained sequential comparison and delayed multi-horizon feedback already have a direct method. Retaining the optimum differs from certifying every retained method. |
| Steven R. Howard, Aaditya Ramdas, Jon McAuliffe, Jasjeet Sekhon, [Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences, 1810.08240v9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240v9), 6 August 2022; [Annals of Statistics 2021](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991) | Selected full text: §4.1, running-intersection discussion, Proposition 9, self-normalization proof | Empirical-Bernstein inference for changing conditional-average means is an established foundation. Substituting sample variance into a Gaussian boundary does not reproduce its guarantee. |
| Sebastian Arnold, Georgios Gavrilopoulos, Benedikt Schulz, Johanna Ziegel, [Sequential model confidence sets, 2404.18678v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18678v4), 22 January 2026 | Additional selected full text: Theorem 3.4 and §3.4 | Predictable normalization can change the across-time ranking target. Dynamic superiority and permanent elimination need different interpretations. |
| Alexander Henzi and Johanna F. Ziegel, [Correction to: ‘Valid sequential inference on probability forecast performance’](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac043), first online 13 September 2022 | Complete official two-page correction, including proof and counterexample | Delayed forecasts require explicit information and stopping rules. The original unadjusted averaged process is generally not a full-filtration supermartingale when the forecast horizon exceeds one. |
| Carole Bernard and Silvana M. Pesenti, [Preference robust distortion risk measures, 2608.02854v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02854v1), 3 August 2026 | Selected full text: §§1–4 and selected Appendix A passages | Preference-weight uncertainty differs from uncertainty in the loss distribution. General signed weights and coherent risk weights have different restrictions. |
| Peng Liu, Steven Vanduffel, Yi Xia, Jing Yao, [Robust distortion risk metrics with Wasserstein and Unimodal Constraints, 2511.08662v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08662v2), revised 27 September 2026 | Selected full text: definitions, §§3–5; appendix proofs not fully audited | Loss-law shape restrictions, known moments, and portfolio ambiguity must be distinguished. The inspected portfolio examples are synthetic, and the portfolio formulation does not incorporate every earlier unimodality restriction. |
| Wei Wang and Huifu Xu, [Preference robust distortion risk measure and its application](https://doi.org/10.1111/mafi.12379), Mathematical Finance 33(2), 389–434 (2023) | Selected full text: elicitation sets, reformulation statements, allocation example and scenario construction | Preference elicitation with concave/inverse-S distortions and insurance capital allocation is already a direct economic application. A return series alone does not identify investor preferences. |
| Qiuqi Wang, Ruodu Wang, Johanna Ziegel, [E-backtesting, 2209.00991v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00991v6), revised 15 April 2026 | Additional selected full text: §§7.2, 8.1–8.2 and D.6 | Structural-change monitoring and a dynamically optimized 22-stock portfolio are already examined. One portfolio stream differs from simultaneous guarantees for individual institutions. |
| Jaehyeok Shin, Aaditya Ramdas, Alessandro Rinaldo, [E-detectors: A Nonparametric Framework for Sequential Change Detection](https://doi.org/10.51387/23-NEJSDS51), NEJSDS 2(2), 229–260 (2024) | Additional selected full text: §§2.2–2.5, late-change discussion, §§6.2–6.3 and ARL proof | Start-date mixtures, e-SR/e-CUSUM recursions, and summable-weight alternatives are mature tools. No-change average alarm time and probability of any false alarm are different targets. |
| Timo Dimitriadis and Yannick Hoga, [Systemic Risk Surveillance, 2601.08598v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08598v1), 13 January 2026 | Selected full text: §§2.1–2.2, Proposition 1, Theorem 1, Algorithm 1, §3.4, §4 | Multiple-institution monitoring already has a direct financial precedent. A finite monitoring window is different from a multi-day forecast horizon or indefinite monitoring. |

The next reading follows these papers' reference chains, including the original financial VaR/ES monitoring theorem, preference-robust foundations, and the corrected conditions for joint VaR/ES scoring. Abstract-only leads remain separate from theorem inputs. No absence-of-results search is treated as proof of novelty, and no raw third-party data or paper PDFs are redistributed.
