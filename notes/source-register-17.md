## 1. Realized variation as information for a future return density

**John M. Maheu and Thomas H. McCurdy (2011), “Do high-frequency measures of volatility improve forecasts of return distributions?” Journal of Econometrics 160(1), 69–76, January 2011.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2010.03.016); [institutional publication record](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/315790). An exact first-online day was not independently certified.

**Actual body/scope:** [University of Toronto Working Paper 324](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/public/workingPapers/tecipa-324.pdf), 31 PDF pages, with university cover dated **6 August 2008** and internal author title page dated **June 2008**. After the cover, printed page equals PDF page minus one. Recorded root reading selected PDF pp.1–21: model definitions, data construction, variance links, alternative dynamics/innovations, density calculation, forecast targets, selected interpretation and the first descriptive table. An earlier selected reading also inspected the density-target page visually. Remaining result tables/figures/references, the final eight-page journal body, external measurement/inference proofs and implementation were not comprehensively reviewed. No full draft-to-journal equivalence is asserted.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_START -->
Maheu and McCurdy use realized variation within joint models that issue return-density forecasts. Their June 2008 manuscript distinguishes a conditional-variance link through expected variation from a stronger observable-volatility specification. HAR and component dynamics for log variation, together with separate return and variation innovations, determine different predictive laws. Multi-step calculation integrates future states and averages available conditional densities. Its evaluated target is a future single day's return at each horizon; it is not the cumulative return over that horizon. Common verification dates keep the observed outcomes comparable across horizons, while dependence still requires separate inference assumptions. Applications to SPY and IBM compare predictive log scores with an EGARCH benchmark and report specification-dependent improvements. The realized measure supplies forecast information rather than replacing the observed verifying return. This already establishes a financial density-forecasting application. A point variance forecast alone does not determine the required return law. The final journal body, precise parameter-update schedule, external proofs, original observations and executable replication remain unreviewed.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** retain the chosen joint innovation law, information available at the forecast origin, log-to-level moment adjustment and the conditional variance/measurement qualifications. The stronger same-day scale equation does not reveal a future realized measure to today's forecaster. HAR, component models and EGARCH are existing comparators. The draft's terminal-day density target differs from the cumulative-return simulation in Realized GARCH and the moment/path-law distinction in HEAVY. Comparative log-score inference does not establish financial utility, simultaneous horizon validity or optional-stopping control.

**Application, access and rights:** the draft uses adjusted intraday TAQ transaction measures for SPY and IBM, ending 29 August 2007, with asset-specific starts. Its evaluated horizons share target dates; the exact rolling-versus-expanding estimation rule and update frequency were not certified. Current TAQ access, redistribution permissions and a paper-specific program archive/license were not verified. Public university access to the manuscript does not establish permissions for the paper, observations or separate software. No original observations or executable program was obtained.

## 2. HAR predicts a specified volatility measurement

**Fulvio Corsi (2009), “A Simple Approximate Long-Memory Model of Realized Volatility.” Journal of Financial Econometrics 7(2), 174–196.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbp001); [actual public university-hosted journal-layout copy](https://statmath.wu.ac.at/~hauser/LVs/FinEtricsQF/References/Corsi2009JFinEtrics_LMmodelRealizedVola.pdf). The actual title page prints **Advance Access publication 19 February 2009**. Production and library-download timestamps are not author revision dates.

**Actual body/scope:** 23 PDF pages, printed pp.174–196; printed page equals PDF page plus 173. Recorded root reading selected passages across pp.1–23, emphasizing pp.3–8 definitions/cascade/measurement error, pp.11–16 persistence and estimation, and pp.17–20 forecasting. PDF p.4, printed p.177, was visually inspected for units and aggregation. Captions and interpretation were read without auditing every curve or table cell. The external diffusion/proxy, OLS/HAC and long-memory proofs, original observations and programs were not independently reviewed. Historical earlier titles are not treated as editions actually read.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_2_START -->
Corsi motivates a parsimonious autoregression through volatility components at daily, weekly and monthly horizons. The original response is realized volatility in standard-deviation units, and its longer components average daily volatility levels. Variance-scale and logarithmic adaptations therefore change the forecast object rather than merely its notation. Expanding the overlapping components yields a restricted finite autoregression that can mimic persistent behavior over observed scales. The article explicitly distinguishes this approximation from asymptotic fractional long memory. Its latent-to-observed argument also separates consistent measurement from the stronger mean-zero measurement-error premise. Applications cover exchange rates, equity-index futures and Treasury futures. Rolling HAR forecasts use daily reestimation, while the authors qualify a fractional benchmark that retains a full-sample parameter. Longer-horizon evaluation aggregates volatility measurements; it does not issue a complete return-density forecast. These established point-forecast applications provide a close predecessor for later realized-measure models. Reported accuracy does not certify proxy-robust ranking or trading value. External proofs, exact implementation, original observations and current data/code permissions remain unverified.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_2_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** retain the source's units, positive-component construction, innovation assumptions, measurement qualification and stable autoregressive specification. An average of daily standard deviations, a sum of daily variances and the standard deviation of a longer return are different objects. The heterogeneous-horizon interpretation is not causal identification of trader groups. Fractional models, MIDAS and later log-variation/variance HAR implementations are relevant neighbors, with different restrictions. The already registered signed-variation application and the other joint models here extend different targets rather than establish a new generic HAR method.

**Application, access and rights:** the source uses USD/CHF midpoint quotes, S&P500 futures and long-term U.S. Treasury futures, with different historical samples and two-scale measurements. Its rolling HAR window has 1000 observations; the source explicitly qualifies the fractional benchmark's full-sample integration estimate. Exact futures construction, implementation indices, a current replication archive and data/program licenses were not verified. The journal copy's copyright and public university availability do not grant separate redistribution permission for Reuters, futures observations or unlocated software. No observations or program package was acquired.

## 3. A measurement equation completes a joint future model

**Peter Reinhard Hansen, Zhuo Huang and Howard Howan Shek (2012), “Realized GARCH: A Joint Model for Returns and Realized Measures of Volatility.” Journal of Applied Econometrics 27(6), 877–906, September 2012.** First online **17 March 2011**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.1234); [author research page](https://reinhardhansen.github.io/research.html).

**Actual body/scope:** the [public Duke university course-library PDF](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~get/browse/courses/201/spr12/DOWNLOADS/WorkingPapers_Now_Published/phs_realized_garch_10.pdf), an early-online Wiley-typeset article with 2011 footers and self-numbered pp.1–30. These actual pages are not mapped automatically to final issue pp.877–906. Recorded root reading selected PDF pp.1–12, 17, 19–27 and 28–30, emphasizing information/measurement structure, estimation scope, empirical split, return versus joint likelihood, multi-period forecasting, cumulative returns and selected appendix mechanisms. PDF pp.4, 11 and 23 were visually inspected. Final issue equivalence, the separate Web Appendix, external QMLE/stochastic-recurrence proofs and every matrix/product/result cell were not independently certified.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_3_START -->
Hansen, Huang and Shek specify returns together with a measurement equation for realized variation. Conditional return variance is predictable from past returns and measurements; the contemporaneous measure can differ systematically in scale and respond to the return shock. Modeling that relationship completes future dynamics needed for multi-step prediction. The log-linear specification keeps variance positive and yields useful autoregressive representations under its restrictions. The source separates return-only likelihood comparisons from joint likelihood gains obtained by modeling an additional measurement. Its financial exercise estimates on 2002–2007 and evaluates during January–August 2008 for stocks and SPY. Cumulative-return distributions and their asymmetric moments are already illustrated through model simulation. They are not empirical calibration of every path-risk functional. In the actual early-online body, score and Hessian limit components retain explicit assumptions, while a complete estimator limit theorem is left for further work. This mature joint-model framework does not provide universal measurement-error robustness. Final-version equivalence, supplementary proofs, executable programs, original observations and redistribution rights remain unverified.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_3_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** the base model specifies return and measurement innovations, their moment/independence structure and a leverage function. Log-state stationarity does not automatically establish all variance-level moments. Likelihood-gradient conditions are separate from conditions for out-of-sample predictive-score comparisons. A fitted measurement equation does not establish conditional-unbiased-proxy ranking for arbitrary observations. Maheu–McCurdy, MEM/HEAVY and conventional GARCH are close alternatives. Future-path generation and cumulative-return moments are established here; a new asset or path functional alone does not establish a new method or a calibrated risk forecast.

**Application, access and rights:** the actual body studies 28 stocks and SPY, realized kernels and distinct trading-hours versus close-to-close returns. Its earlier-estimation/later-evaluation split differs from daily rolling reestimation in the other applications. The source's cumulative-return illustration is model generated, rather than a drawdown backtest or portfolio-profit result. Original measurement data, current program availability, cleaning/short-session implementation and software/data reuse permissions were not independently verified. The public typeset copy is evidence for reading, not a separate data or program license. No observations or executable archive was obtained.

## 4. Conditional moments and a predictive path law require different assumptions

**Neil Shephard and Kevin Sheppard (2010), “Realising the future: forecasting with high-frequency-based volatility (HEAVY) models.” Journal of Applied Econometrics 25(2), 197–231, March 2010.** First published **27 January 2010**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.1158); [official formal HTML](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jae.1158); [author publication page](https://shephard.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/realising-future-forecasting-high-frequency-based-volatility-heavy-models).

**Actual body/scope:** selected formal HTML and the [author-linked manuscript](https://shephard.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum7741/files/heavy.pdf), dated **10 July 2009**, parsed by the reader as 41 pages. Recorded root selections cover formal §§1–2.4.5, §4.3 and adjoining interpretation, and the cleaning appendix; principal reader ranges are 15–68, 96–173, 196–436, 697–700, 741–768 and 880–918. Author-reader selections cover central definitions, Theorem1's short argument and bootstrap passages around pp.5–9 and 15–17. The completed supporting reading additionally covers selected application/data prose. Equations rendered as images in the final HTML were read through author-manuscript text, without full version equivalence. A normal direct PDF request failed; the retained hashes identify reader-text derivatives, **not original PDF or HTML bytes**. No usable root screenshot verification, external estimation/bootstrap proof or complete numerical audit is claimed.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_4_START -->
Shephard and Sheppard distinguish predictable close-to-close return variance from the conditional mean of a trading-hours realized measure. Their two linked recursions accommodate scale differences and propagate multi-step conditional moments. Horizon-specific fitting and integrated variants are established alternatives within the framework. Ordinary quasi-likelihood arguments use their conditional-moment restrictions, while targeting estimated unconditional means changes the covariance treatment. A full predictive distribution requires more information: the authors add independently and identically distributed innovation pairs and resample the paired residuals to propagate future returns and measurements. Pairing retains contemporaneous dependence; conditional unit means alone do not justify this path bootstrap. Financial evaluation uses a historical realized library and rolling forecasts, distinguishing individual-horizon and cumulative comparisons. Cumulative variance still does not identify a complete return or drawdown distribution. The framework already includes financial future-input dynamics and path generation. These constructions remain model dependent. Selected formal text and an earlier manuscript were read; external asymptotic proofs, implementation, original observations and present processed-data/software permissions remain unreviewed.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_4_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** retain the separate return/RM information sets and scales, conditional innovation moments and parameter restrictions. The weak second-order representation adds residual-moment conditions; integrated boundary variants do not inherit every stationary targeting argument. Aggregate-return variance needs the stated cross-moment restrictions, and paired path simulation uses a stronger innovation-law premise. Quasi-likelihood estimation, temporal forecast comparison and path-risk calibration are separate guarantees. Earlier multiplicative-error systems, Brownlees–Gallo, Maheu–McCurdy and Realized GARCH are existing neighbors; no unread neighboring proof is certified through this comparison.

**Application, access and rights:** the historical Oxford-Man library version 0.1 covers 34 index/exchange-rate series with asset-dependent starts. The source uses rolling 1008-observation windows, daily updates and one- through 22-step forecasts. Its Reuters raw and cleaned base observations are expressly unavailable from the authors. Present processed-library permissions are a separate unresolved question. The paper's archive-directory route was inaccessible; no file inventory, README, software version or license was read. Free Access and an author attachment do not grant separate data/program redistribution rights. No observations, executable archive or simulation was obtained or run.
