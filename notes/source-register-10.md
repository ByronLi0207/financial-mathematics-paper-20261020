# Source register 10: drawdown pricing, conditional risk and dependent resampling

20 October 2026. Literature review remains in progress. These are original-source reading records and bounded applicability assessments, not a selected research design or new empirical findings. Recent work is being checked against its methodological foundations and closest financial applications. No popularity ranking is inferred from publication dates.

## 1. Drawdown CAPM: portfolio mapping and the pricing target

**Michael Zabarankin, Konstantin Pavlikov and Stan Uryasev (2014), “Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) with drawdown measure.”** *European Journal of Operational Research* 234(2), 508–517. DOI [10.1016/j.ejor.2013.03.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2013.03.024). Online 27 March 2013; April 2014 issue. Actual source: journal-typeset author copy, linked by [Uryasev's official publication list](https://uryasev.github.io/publications/), entry 44; SHA-256 `c58595f9829237dfd050fa69b69f9a44623fa4a48a66e853700478690a06e02b`.

**Depth:** selected full text, displayed definitions, pricing argument and financial application; not independent certification of every proof step. The cumulative portfolio path is linear in initial holdings. Compounded constituent prices fit that map, but a periodically rebalanced strategy needs its own mapping. Scenario-by-date CDaR and an optimized portfolio's pricing relation are separate objects from population confidence. The reported market-proxy calculation assumes a connection to an optimizer; it is not an unconditional market law. Existing HFRX/AORDA hedge-fund examples already apply drawdown pricing and hedging. The historical case-study entry advertised code/data/results, but one present-day visit retrieved no case documentation, solver specification or license. Neither current replication success nor redistribution permission is established.

## 2. Extremal-score forecast dominance: the null and its contact set

**Yu-Min Yen and Tso-Jung Yen (2021), “Testing forecast accuracy of expectiles and quantiles with the extremal consistent loss functions.”** *International Journal of Forecasting* 37(2), 733–758. DOI [10.1016/j.ijforecast.2020.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2020.09.004). Actual body read: [arXiv:1707.02048v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02048v3), submitted 15 July 2018, title page 17 July; 69 actual PDF pages. The formal journal body was not read.

**Depth:** selected definitions, assumptions, displayed asymptotic/bootstrap arguments, proof passages and financial examples. Dominance concerns unconditional expected extremal-score differences. Joint forecast/outcome vectors are block-resampled; this does not independently certify arbitrary forecast-model refitting. The binding threshold set can be smaller than the entire domain. Calibration at the all-thresholds-equal least-favorable configuration is not equal rejection probability at every boundary law. Bounded-domain proof passages, mixing/moment/density premises and nondegenerate limiting distributions retain their qualifications. Existing equity-premium and S&P500 VaR comparisons are direct financial predecessors. Nonrejection is not proof of dominance. No new bootstrap theorem or complete independent proof validation is claimed.

## 3. Stochastic dominance: subsampling is a different procedure

**Oliver Linton, Esfandiar Maasoumi and Yoon-Jae Whang (2005), “Consistent Testing for Stochastic Dominance under General Sampling Schemes.”** *Review of Economic Studies* 72(3), 735–765. DOI [10.1111/j.1467-937X.2005.00350.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-937X.2005.00350.x). Actual body: [official FMG Discussion Paper 508](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP508.pdf), dated 9 December 2003, first version April 2001; 51 pages, SHA-256 `e4fa5475bdf0bdb131602cb3390fbc930ce96ac2b8b35cda50ca8b64e1c4e3f1`. Formal 2005 body unread.

**Depth:** selected hypotheses, nuisance-parameter expansion, subsampling theorem/proof and alternative-bootstrap discussion. The principal procedure uses shorter consecutive segments, re-estimates parameters within each segment and scales by the segment length. It differs from full-length centered bootstrap resampling. Estimated parameters contribute influence terms under the source's conditions. Its admissible data-dependent subsample-size result is not uniform validity over arbitrary data-generating laws. Utility/payoff-distribution stochastic dominance also differs from forecast-score dominance. The full-sample bootstrap alternative is a separate qualified sketch. Existing stock-return applications preclude treating routine financial stochastic-dominance testing as new merely through another return series.

## 4. Conditional VaR/AVaR regression: fitting and approximation

**So Yeon Chun, Alexander Shapiro and Stan Uryasev (2012), “Conditional Value-at-Risk and Average Value-at-Risk: Estimation and Asymptotics.”** *Operations Research* 60(4), 739–756. DOI [10.1287/opre.1120.1072](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1120.1072). Actual source: 18-page journal-typeset author copy, [official publication list](https://uryasev.github.io/publications/) entry 48; SHA-256 `e799da7356ff97a5b775b4d7773a8d4a29691fad5a828217834bb09b26b86453`.

**Depth:** selected model, estimation, consistency/asymptotic arguments, appendix derivations and financial examples; simulation passages read selectively. “Conditional” concerns predictors; AVaR names the tail mean. The model uses a linear location structure with errors independent of predictors and iid sampling. Residual risk correction and coupled mixed-quantile regression are existing methods. At fixed grid size, a quantile mixture approximates AVaR; sample growth alone does not remove quadrature error. SAA here fits regression parameters and offsets, rather than certifying searched portfolio decisions. Atom-compatible AVaR definitions do not remove the extra assumptions of normal approximations. Residual-covariance details were not independently certified. CDS spread-change, IBM and airline examples already exist; their vendor observations and implementation timing were not reproduced. The conclusion leaves dynamic time-series extensions for later work.

## 5. Stationary bootstrap: scalar distributions and whole processes

**Dimitris N. Politis and Joseph P. Romano (1994), “The Stationary Bootstrap.”** *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 89(428), 1303–1313. DOI [10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870). Actual source: [University of Wisconsin journal scan](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~behansen/718/Politis%20Romano.pdf), 11 pages; SHA-256 `269f5437920af4861360d4e1a74a77f3aa6a3d3f070cd52a9f0e8502822a5c35`. December 1994 publication differs from the 2012 digital-platform date.

**Depth:** actual page-image reading of the algorithm, main theorems, extensions and selected proofs. Theorem 2 concerns one scalar sample mean under stationarity, covariance/cumulant summability, moments, mixing and block-rate conditions. Its supremum is over a scalar CDF argument. Theorems 3–4 concern fixed-dimensional means and smooth functions. Section 4.3 separately discusses process tightness, mentions the stronger rate `N*p_N^2 -> infinity`, and defers technical details. Neither a finite empirical knot calculation nor fixed-threshold convergence alone certifies a whole score process. Conditional stationarity of generated resamples is not validity for arbitrary observed laws or fitted statistics. This record does not repair or invalidate a later paper's proof.
