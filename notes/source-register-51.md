## Original synthesis

Carlier, Dana and Galichon connect risk-sharing efficiency under concave order with scalar and multivariate comonotonicity. For bounded scalar total risk on an atomless space, their improvement theorem yields a comonotone allocation and a strict improvement when the starting allocation is not comonotone. The multivariate construction uses strictly convex allocation costs and limits of joint laws; its efficiency implications have different logical strengths. Selected proofs proceed through a compact law-space problem, duality and a variational sequence, rather than assuming attainment of every auxiliary cost problem. The paper already discusses a numerical efficiency criterion. It supplies neither a calibrated financial forecast test nor a new method merely through asset substitution. The retained repository copy has distinct deposit and title-page dates. This record covers selected statements and supplied proof routes, with external originals and the formal journal body unread.

## Premises and limits

| Object | Conditions and interpretation | Limit for financial use |
|---|---|---|
| Concave dominance | Smaller expectations for all convex tests; component means preserved. Strict dominance and strict convexity differ. | Not larger expected gains, measured preferences or guaranteed tail improvement under changed beliefs. |
| Scalar improvement, Theorem 3.1 | Bounded total risk; atomless space; bounded components adding to the total. Increasing 1-Lipschitz functions represent comonotone allocations. | Atomless space is not an atomless aggregate law or independence from any given vector. Positivity, caps, trading constraints and extra budgets are not automatic. |
| Scalar efficiency, Theorem 3.2 | Suitable strictly convex costs exist; compactness uses bounded aggregate support, up to zero-sum cash translations. | Costs are not observed preferences. No conditional-law or financial-filtration extension established. |
| Multivariate structure | Several imperfectly substitutable goods/risks; common reference measure, bounded components, adding up; strictly convex costs and weak joint-law limits with a quantified strictly convex baseline. | Not separate scalar asset transformations or arbitrary gradients. Scalar compactness and convexity need not transfer. |
| Theorem 4.6 | Strict w-comonotonicity implies efficiency; efficiency implies the limiting notion; a separate weak-closure relation is stated. | Different logical strengths, not an unrestricted iff. |
| Variational route | Joint laws preserve the aggregate law and meet marginal concave-order constraints. Compact support, bounded directions and strict-convexity uniqueness support the supplied derivative/limit route. | Attainment of the law problem does not establish attainment of the auxiliary convex-function minimization. |

The dual route uses continuous-function spaces with uniform topology, marginal/aggregate adjoints and an infimal-convolution envelope. Fenchel–Rockafellar, Ekeland and other external originals remain unread. The variational principle supplies an approximating sequence; minimizing costs are not assumed bounded or convergent. Compactness concerns generated laws; strict-convexity moduli support strictness. No finite solver, tolerance or runtime rate is certified.
