## Science synthesis — 122 words

<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_START -->
Nolde and Ziegel distinguish calibration restrictions from comparative accuracy. Predictable instruments test selected identification moments, not every conditioning direction. Comparison specifies the benchmark's information; the implemented limiting-average score null differs from conditional dominance. DM/HAC inference retains moment, dependence and variance premises and does not provide finite-sample or anytime validity. Rankings concern a chosen consistent score, not dominance across all scores; score and score-difference homogeneity have distinct domains. Their simulations and NASDAQ application already show instrument sensitivity and short-window rank instability. A method can rank well by joint scores yet fail a general calibration test; a selected quiet window can even rank the oracle adversely. These are unreproduced author findings. Revisiting this mature evidence supplies no new gap, project result, formula or method.
