# WP6 internal review B — industry risk manager

Scope: same-model internal review of the current manuscript, Supplement, separate technical appendix, and the frozen WP1–WP3 readouts/protocols. No Claude or other model was called. This review performs no new calculation, model run, or test and changes no manuscript or scientific result. Exactly seven substantive issues follow. Severity refers to the proposed operational use, not a claim that a candidly disclosed limitation invalidates the retrospective forecasting study.

The current paper already rejects a permanent history threshold, assesses the entire matched-loss curve, distinguishes error preferences from observed costs, and identifies the familiar financial sample. Those disclosures are appropriate. The issues below concern the practical gaps that remain after those disclosures; editing can bound the claim but cannot complete the missing validation.

## B1 — Operational rule: a set of marginal reports does not yet define a coherent limit policy

- **Severity:** High for adopting the reports as an operating limit system; a bounded forecasting result remains valid.
- **Exact location:** Main §3, “The forecast target” (`sec:target`, lines 85–116), especially the horizon-order discussion at line 116; §8, “Implications for model validation” (`sec:implications`, lines 327–336); §10 (`sec:conclusion`, lines 371–377).
- **Evidence:** The report covers the sampled peak including the current position; the text explicitly separates that report from an optimal operating limit. However, the independently fitted short-history FX reports cross across horizons on **640 origins**, although their actual targets are nested. A risk desk using both horizons therefore needs a rule for reconciling the two displayed limits, assigning responsibility, and responding when either is exceeded. A monotonicity repair or subsequent intervention would define a new operating procedure whose loss and breach behavior have not been evaluated here. Reanchoring a report to the current position does not by itself supply that procedure.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Keep the observed crossing result adjacent to the practical discussion and state that the present comparison evaluates the named marginal monitoring reports. Describe joint horizon coherence and a specified response to an exceedance as outstanding conditions for using them in a multi-horizon limit workflow. Do not silently sort or project the reports, call such a repair tested, or equate the known-floor proposition with an optimal limit policy.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** No evaluated intervention, horizon-reconciliation policy, inventory response, or counterfactual operating path exists in the reported evidence. Those require a separately defined and evaluated decision procedure; no additional calculation is requested or performed in WP6.

## B2 — Threshold stability: the observed cutpoints cannot authorize entry, exit, or retuning rules

- **Severity:** High for a cutoff-based implementation; already substantially disclosed in the paper.
- **Exact location:** Main §7, “History-length patterns and their limits” (lines 303–314), §8 (line 336), §9 (`sec:limitations`, line 362); Supplement “History-coordinate discovery and pre-run prediction tests” (`sup:v9-history`); frozen `protocol/revision-v9/wp1-posthoc-design.md` and `protocol/revision-v9/wp3-learning-curve.md`.
- **Evidence:** The selected coordinate is **W/(H+1)** and omits coefficient count. WP1's `breakeven.json` contains more than one crossing for the smaller regression and many bootstrap draws with multiple crossings; the envelopes condition on the selected coordinate and discovery grid. WP3's `curve-predictions.json` records one compatible but left-censored first-cutpoint prediction, and its saved contrasts show both longer-horizon regressions losing again at the largest history. The main text correctly preserves these facts. A manager still cannot infer when to switch the regression on, when to switch it off, or how much estimation history to retain merely from the first crossing.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Retain the full-curve recommendation and say explicitly that the cutpoint is a diagnostic description rather than a model-activation, retirement, or retuning trigger. Keep the left-censored case untestable and the overall financial prediction only partially supported; do not replace the first crossing with a later one or treat the finite-root envelope as a permanent operating range.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** The study does not evaluate a rule for changing model/history in response to a moving cutpoint, its switching costs, or stability on new financial outcomes. A different sentence cannot make the threshold transportable or supply those outcomes.

## B3 — Breaches and costs: score improvement does not determine the desk's service or loss constraint

- **Severity:** High for economic or capacity-allocation claims; the current forecast-score claims can remain.
- **Exact location:** Main §5 (`sec:methods`, line 138), §7 “Where conditional models improve” and Table `tab:fx`, §7 “Scores and breach behavior answer different questions” and Table `tab:diagnostics`, §8 (line 330), §9 (line 364), §10 (lines 374–377); technical appendix “Working-reference inference” (`sup:implementation`, lines 209–217).
- **Evidence:** The later short-horizon long-history matched FX regression has a **14.8%** score reduction but **376 versus 360** breaches. The separate delayed-wrapper comparison has **329 versus 328** breaches and its own score result. The nineteen-to-one weighting is hypothetical. A breach count, a quantile score, and a transition-likelihood diagnostic answer different questions; none fixes an institution's tolerance for a clustered series of interventions, the duration of a liquidity shortage, or its monetary consequence. The paper correctly states that working-reference nonrejection is not proof of conditional coverage.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Continue displaying the score and counts together and prevent the wrapper result from being presented as a repair of the unwrapped procedure. In the operational implication, require an externally specified monitoring objective and service constraint before interpreting one model as preferable for a desk. Label monetary savings, funding benefits, and intervention reductions as unmeasured rather than translating the percentage score change into them.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** Real funding/hedging/intervention prices, operational breach tolerances, and realized consequences under an explicit policy are absent. Choosing a different asymmetric weight after inspecting the results would not supply them and is not an authorized WP6 remedy.

## B4 — Clock and availability: mature labels are necessary but do not establish a live information set

- **Severity:** High for a release-time or calendar-time implementation.
- **Exact location:** Main §3 (`sec:target`, lines 85–92), §4 (`sec:data`, lines 123–131), §5 (line 138), §6 (`sec:evaluation`, lines 174–190), §9 (line 359); Supplement “Reproduction, availability and retained extended results” (`sup:reproduction`); `protocol/revision-20261023/prospective-public-release-protocol.md` and `prospective-vintage-amendment-20261023.md`.
- **Evidence:** The FX experiment counts source records; the daily proxy uses the last record in each nonempty source-naive day, with unknown timezone and historical release vintage. Consequently, the displayed hourly/daily terminology does not establish that each H or W is a fixed elapsed-time span, a market close, or the largest intrarecord exposure. The weekly study indexes a downloaded vintage by economic date, whereas the prospective protocol recognizes publication lag and an unpublished-measurement nowcast/forecast mixture. The observed floor in that release workflow is the captured published aggregate, not necessarily a desk's contemporaneous position. Complete-label eligibility prevents a particular look-ahead error but cannot reconstruct the unavailable historical publication information.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Maintain separate labels for record-clock, source-naive-day, downloaded-vintage, and public-release tasks wherever a practical application is described. State that carrying the same H/W numerals to a live desk does not preserve elapsed-time exposure or information availability. Use the prospective amendment as a proposed clock definition, not evidence that first-release historical replay or live issuance has been achieved.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** Unknown timezone, intrarecord excursions, unavailable first-release vintages, and actual live capture/issue timestamps require observations or implementation. They cannot be inferred from correct chronological indexing or repaired by relabelling existing rows.

## B5 — Transfer: related aggregates and repeated points do not establish dealer-level transport

- **Severity:** High for cross-institution adoption; disclosed adverse evidence should remain central.
- **Exact location:** Main §4 (`sec:data`, lines 126–131), §7 “Where conditional models lose” and Table `tab:weekly`, §7 controlled/within-record extensions including Figure `fig:mechanism` (lines 299–319), §9 (lines 356–359); Supplement complete weekly panel (`sup:panel`) and same-dealer aggregation (`sup:v8-daily`).
- **Evidence:** The financial evidence comprises one FX market maker and related signed primary-dealer position-category aggregates. Cancellation across dealers, shared reporting, and related economic components prevent those aggregates from serving as compatible unseen dealer replicas. Same-dealer daily aggregation changes the measurement/clock as well as multiple forecasting settings. The figure retains repeated same-period FX cells, so its provenance point count is not a count of independent financial validations. Expanded penalties still lose **34 of 48** additional-panel comparisons, and historical headroom wins **7 of 8** daily comparisons. Those failures constrain transfer; they are not noise to omit from a deployment case.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Preserve the independent-dealer gap and identify weekly and daily results as cross-task stress evidence. Keep source identity and repeated-period markers visible; do not use the number of plotted points, panel categories, or overlapping FX windows as the effective number of independent financial replications. The paper can responsibly recommend validating the actual institution and target, without claiming that such validation has occurred.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** A compatible independent dealer record with an adequately defined position and information clock is missing. More restatement of the aggregate results cannot fill that empirical gap or establish gross-exposure/settlement relevance.

## B6 — Same-W reference: a controlled contrast does not select the available operational benchmark

- **Severity:** Moderate to high for model procurement or replacement decisions; the matched comparison itself is appropriate.
- **Exact location:** Main §2 (`sec:literature`, history-curve comparison), §6 (`sec:evaluation`, lines 177–190), §7 Table `tab:fx` and history-length discussion (lines 303–319), §8 (line 327); frozen `protocol/revision-v9/wp3-learning-curve.md`.
- **Evidence:** WP3 changes W for both the regression and its matched historical headroom reference. A crossing therefore compares two jointly changing procedures; it does not isolate regression estimation cost or optimize the desk's existing historical benchmark. The earlier FX result illustrates the operational importance: the regression improves against its matched long-history reference while losing against the original shorter-history rolling reference. The manuscript already keeps those references distinct, which is essential rather than a nuisance to simplify away.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** Keep both the matched design comparison and the named original operational comparison in the adoption discussion. Say that the history curve describes relative performance under a specified reference, rather than choosing the reference or a globally best W. Any manager-facing recommendation should first name the benchmark actually available to the desk; do not let a favorable matched result silently supersede a stronger existing control.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** No prospective rule has been evaluated for selecting W, changing the update schedule, or choosing among the available reference procedures. The matched experiment cannot identify that rule without additional policy/selection evaluation, which is outside this review.

## B7 — Deployment and future validation: batch eligibility is not issue-by-issue operational continuity

- **Severity:** High; a concrete implementation and prospective-evidence blocker, already acknowledged in the technical material.
- **Exact location:** Technical appendix “Numerical eligibility” (`sup:implementation`, lines 196–207); Main §6 “Research design and transparency” (lines 183–190), §9 (line 364), §10 (line 377), “Data and code availability” (lines 382–387); prospective amendment, “Implementation boundary: protocol only.”
- **Evidence:** The solver/candidate contracts preserve numerical failures and prohibit replacement penalties or shortened scores. This is sound experiment accounting, but the technical appendix expressly says that no production continuity or fallback policy is evaluated. The prospective amendment further states that the frozen functions are complete-data batch functions and that a release-capture, missing-week/refit-continuation adapter has not been implemented or validated. Its fixed calendar issue slots, late/missed capture treatment and failed-refit behavior are plans, not a completed service or a set of issued forecasts. Public packaging/readback and runtime receipts establish their stated artifact/numerical properties, not live availability or future forecast performance.
- **Writing / existing-evidence response:** In the practical conclusion, preserve the separation between candidate forecasting evidence and readiness to issue operational reports. Reference the prospective protocol as an unexecuted plan and list live capture, failure treatment, fixed-parameter issuance and eventual matured outcomes as remaining milestones. Do not describe numerical PASS statuses or a reproducible public package as a deployment-readiness or prospective-success certificate.
- **Gap that prose cannot solve:** The adapter, operational failure/continuity behavior, actual issuance receipts, future outcomes, and compatible external evaluation have not been supplied. They require implementation and elapsed future observations under the frozen protocol, not another retrospective rewrite or an invented fallback.

## Overall risk-manager assessment

The current paper is suitable for a bounded retrospective model-validation discussion: it makes adverse comparisons visible and no longer supplies a universal permanent threshold claim. None of the seven points requires changing a saved number or weakening a negative result. The most material remaining practical blockers are the unevaluated coherent action policy, the mismatch between historical measurement clocks and live availability, missing compatible external dealer evidence, and unimplemented prospective operational continuity. Clearer scope can prevent misuse; it cannot make those gaps PASS.
