The current regime DGP has fixed phi, zero conditional mean and an exogenous two-state innovation standard deviation. It is a simple discrete level-process robustness condition within the mature Markov-volatility tradition, not a reproduction of Hamilton1989's changing-mean model or the full SWARCH specification. These distinctions follow from comparing the frozen equations with the cited frameworks, not from a new empirical test.

The jump DGP uses Gaussian plus symmetric compound-Poisson additive innovations in a mean-reverting level process. Merton's cited model concerns continuous-time geometric stock prices and multiplicative jumps with option valuation. No Merton price model or pricing conclusion is implemented or claimed here. Normalizing the specified independent innovation variance to1 retains the5:1 elementary-jump/Gaussian standard-deviation ratio; it does not make a finite-variance distribution thin-tailed or imply a conditional forecasting gain.

The Student normalization is supported by the NIST variance formula nu/(nu−2) for the finite-variance case; t5 therefore scales bysqrt(3/5). The compound-Poisson variance1.25 is a direct calculation under the explicitly specified independent Gaussian variance1, event intensity0.01 and symmetric event variance25. These algebraic facts do not constitute a new financial theorem.

Any journal citation uses the publisher's1989,25(1),3–22 and DOI90094-9; no inference about popularity or quality is made from recency or these citations.

## Pre-outcome interpretation boundary

The primary agent also read the Hamilton author-draft bodypp1–5 (equations1–12) and the Merton original introduction/§2 excerpt through journalp128 while the formal run remained active. These are partial-body checks, not claims of reading either complete work. The initial-state and jump-construction distinctions above agree with those primary passages.

Independently of whether the frozen screen will pass, these new DGPs test the robustness of the existing forecasting procedures. They do not include a true conditional-quantile oracle, nor do they hold penalty menus identical between model sizes. A successful finite-grid history pattern would therefore not separate estimation cost from approximation error, regularization or procedure differences. The allowed computation list contains no new oracle experiment; this limitation must be retained rather than silently introducing one. Unit unconditional innovation variance also does not equate every DGP's conditional peak-limit distribution. No claim of a new switching/jump model or a universal sample-size theorem follows from this construction.
