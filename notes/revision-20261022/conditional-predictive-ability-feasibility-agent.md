## Original result needed here

The paper compares forecasting **methods**, retaining estimation uncertainty with bounded estimation memory as evaluation length grows; nested and nonnested comparisons require nondegenerate covariance. For fixed horizon and finite-dimensional issue-known instruments, the full-information null is `E[D(t,H)|F_t]=0`; selected moments use `Z=h_t D`. Theorem3 requires specified mixing rates, moments of `Z`, and uniformly positive-definite covariance. Its covariance sum ends at `H−1` because of the full conditional null, with weights converging to one at each retained lag. Theorem4's unconditional null instead requires a consistent general long-run variance: its bandwidth grows while remaining smaller than the sample length. The displayed moment assumptions and proof uniformity must be retained. These are asymptotic statements, not finite-sample guarantees. General loss functions do not require a continuous positive target density in these theorems. A finite instrument vector tests only selected implications, and nonrejection is not conditional equality or calibrated coverage.

## Application reasoning, not a completed theorem transfer

**A path peak is not automatically excluded.** For each fixed H, define the resolved target at index s by `Y_s^(H)=max_(s−H<=j<=s)|N_j|`. At issue t the existing report concerns `Y_(t+H)^(H)`, which resolves at t+H. Thus a fixed-horizon comparison can be expressed in the paper's issue/resolution notation without substituting a signed endpoint. This is an indexing observation, not a proof that the resulting process satisfies the theorem. A known-floor atom and pinball's kink do not themselves rule out this general-loss approach; the separate conditional-quantile-combination paper's density assumptions must not be imported as a universal prohibition.

**Periodic refits do not inherently invalidate bounded memory.** Conditional on specified fixed tuning settings, the current refit lag is bounded, as are label windows and lag/RMS lookbacks. If r is the last refit, the oldest rich training feature reads `N_(r−H−W+1−L)` and `t−r<=d−1`. The relevant raw-memory bound is therefore `W+H+L+d−1` records, not W alone. This is finite for the declared settings. The deterministic refit phase and recorded-calendar inputs need an explicit method/filtration representation, but shared coefficients do not independently justify a longer covariance cutoff under the *full conditional null*. They can produce longer dependence under an unconditional comparison, for which the general long-run variance remains relevant.

**One-time validation selection is retained state.** The reported lambda/k choices came from an initial validation block and remain fixed. Treating these realized choices as fixed method definitions or conditioning on the selection is a possible analysis route; it still needs an explicit justification for the stochastic/mixing setup. A rolling W-row fit alone does not prove the entire data-dependent procedure has the finite-block representation used by the argument when a random selection from a permanently retained initial block affects every future forecast. This is an unresolved application mapping, not a claim that the paper forbids all fixed fits or all tuning.

**The covariance choice depends on the question.** At H24, using lag23 is justified by the source's full-information conditional null, not merely by observing overlapping24-record labels. In an unconditional mean-loss comparison, neither horizon overlap nor periodic refitting establishes zero covariance after lag23. Also, the source requires retained-lag weights to approach one: a conventional Bartlett weight `1−j/H` with fixed truncation H−1 is constant in n, so that software setting is not by itself the displayed Theorem3 condition. This arithmetic observation is not an investigation of a source error or a recommendation for a particular unverified estimator.

**Current sample and design limits remain material.** The277 Treasury origins at H24, the13-record refit cadence and long history make small-sample covariance and power behavior a serious question. No theorem supplies a numerical sample-size threshold or proves that277 is invalid; nor does thinning to12 origins produce independence or an effective-sample-size estimate. FX has more origins but its evaluation archive was already inspected. The later AR comparisons inspected both sources before design. A post hoc choice of instrument, contrast, bandwidth or stopping point is not covered just by identifying a fixed-pair asymptotic test. Sample inspection also does not disappear when a new executable freeze is published.

**Numerical acceptance does not prove population regularity.** Finite stored forecasts and successful solver/resource checks do not establish the required uniform loss-product moments, mixing rate or nondegenerate long-run covariance. At the exact Treasury k=W fallback, KNN and F have identical stored reports; an all-zero loss difference is not eligible for an invertible-variance Wald test merely because the method labels differ. An unconditional equal-loss test is also not automatically a confidence interval for an arbitrarily time-varying population-average loss advantage. That interval's estimand and centering require their own argument.

## Feasibility decision

Giacomini–White is a **potential framework**, not a presently established license for new confidence intervals or p-values in this manuscript. Overlap, periodic refits, nested descriptions or a floor atom alone do not make inference impossible. What remains unverified is the complete method/selection representation, issue-time filtration, population dependence and moments, covariance nondegeneracy, null-specific variance estimator and finite-sample behavior. Treasury economic-date replay cannot become a live-public-information comparison through this test.

A future inferential protocol would first fix the estimand and null, the exact pair and predictable instrument set, selection/conditioning treatment, variance formula and bandwidth sequence, multiplicity and fixed evaluation rule. Only then could implementation and explicitly assumption-dependent size/power checks be considered. No such protocol was frozen or executed here. These results therefore remain descriptive in the present paper, without claiming that every future time-series inference route is closed.
