## What the case actually measures

Chapter 3's January 2017 case uses client trade amount and direction, open/close information, interval prices, currency-conversion multipliers and client/symbol groupings. Section 3.6, physical p.44, separately defines broker-perspective position and PnL fields. Position involves trade amount, side and a base-currency multiplier; PnL additionally involves price changes and the appropriate conversion. The open, float, close and locked classifications depend on trade start/stop relative to the epoch. A single released signed scalar cannot identify all these fields.

Figure 3.5 illustrates a broker PnL path and its type decomposition; Figure 3.6 illustrates selected client PnL paths; Figure 3.7 displays USD-converted positions grouped by currency symbol. These are different measurements and groupings, not three alternative observations of the present future absolute-peak target. The figures and surrounding discussion illustrate why client/symbol decomposition could inform hedging. They do not themselves execute a future inventory-peak quantile forecast or measure the effect of a changed hedge policy. This conclusion is restricted to the task definitions and figures inspected.

## Consequences for the current direction

It also confirms that richer conditioning, event calendars, separate long/short forecasts and generated position paths were already discussed as extensions.
