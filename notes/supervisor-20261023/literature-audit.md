## Three concise manuscript paragraphs

Dealer inventory creates exposure while customer trades arrive at uncertain times. Ho and Stoll(1981) connect this exposure to optimal quote adjustment, and Lyons(1995) provides transaction-level evidence for inventory control in an FX dealership market. At the market level, Duffie et al.(2023) relate intensive dealer balance-sheet use to impaired Treasury intermediation. These studies motivate monitoring the position held through a horizon. The present empirical object is the peak of sampled source-reported net positions; its measurement is separate from realized funding costs or a complete dealer balance sheet.

Path extrema and liquidity buffers have established financial precedents. Boudoukh et al.(2004) examine portfolio losses reached before the terminal date through MaxVaR. Petrunia et al.(2019) model extreme daily settlement debits, first taking a maximum across payment-system participants. Van den Hoogen(2017) defines a liquidity buffer from a conditional quantile of the largest cumulative payment deficit within an interval. Our target is the two-sided absolute position peak on the observed sampling grid. The comparison fixes completed-label histories and refit schedules across historical and conditional methods, making the incremental contribution an empirical comparison on a common target.

Conditional position prediction and quantile evaluation are also established. Wisniewski et al.(2020) use lagged and calendar information with quantile and conformal intervals for next-hour signed net positions in the same FX source family. We evaluate a multi-record peak and pair the resulting forecasts on common origins. Gneiting(2011) supplies the consistency argument for pinball evaluation; Ehm et al.(2016) explain how rankings can vary within the wider class of scores consistent for the same quantile. We therefore accompany mean pinball loss with underprediction, overprediction and breach diagnostics, and use empirical Murphy diagrams to inspect scoring sensitivity.
