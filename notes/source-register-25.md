## 2. Research paper — recursive filtered paths and derivative repricing

**Giovanni Barone-Adesi, Kostas Giannopoulos and Les Vosper (1999), “VaR without correlations for portfolios of derivative securities.” Journal of Futures Markets19(5),583–602, August1999.** [Formal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/%28SICI%291096-9934%28199908%2919%3A5%3C583%3A%3AAID-FUT5%3E3.0.CO%3B2-S).

**Source synthesis — 147 words.**

<!-- SYNTHESIS_2_START -->
Barone-Adesi, Giannopoulos and Vosper filter returns and recursively generate future market paths from standardized historical innovation vectors. A common historical date preserves each step's empirical cross-asset co-movement; successive dates are sampled anew rather than as consecutive blocks. Simulated shocks update volatility and price states before nonlinear option or swap repricing. Estimated coefficients remain fixed, so state propagation does not incorporate parameter uncertainty. Filter adequacy and innovation assumptions remain substantive. Futures, a call option and hypothetical swaps illustrate terminal portfolio-risk distributions, with some valuation inputs held fixed. This extends beyond daily-percentile square-root scaling, without establishing universal multi-period calibration. Selected March 1999 institutional methods and application passages were read. The 1994–1995 history and 1996 example are source descriptions, not a reconstructed chronological holdout. Full numerical results, external filter/valuation theory, final-edition equivalence, executable materials and current data permissions remain unverified. A scenario law and a pricing map have different roles.

## 3. Research paper — uncertainty in conditional risk estimates

**Peter Christoffersen and Sílvia Gonçalves (2005), “Estimation risk in financial risk management.” Journal of Risk7(3),1–28, Spring/April2005.** [Formal DOI](https://doi.org/10.21314/jor.2005.112).

Actual body: [CIRANO Scientific Series2004s-15](https://cirano.qc.ca/files/publications/2004s-15.pdf), cover **April2004**,30physical pages. The formal2005 body was not read or version-compared. This entry is limited to selected FHS, forecast-origin conditioning and the stated simulation scope.

**Source synthesis — 145 words.**

<!-- SYNTHESIS_3_START -->
Christoffersen and Gonçalves use residual resampling to quantify uncertainty in conditional VaR and expected-shortfall estimates. Pseudo histories generate refitted volatility parameters; original observed losses are then refiltered under those parameters to retain the actual forecast-origin state. FHS also reestimates innovation-tail constants. The target is an unknown conditional risk functional, not tomorrow's random loss or complete calibration of issued forecasts. Correct GARCH specification, iid standardized innovations and the stated parameter regime remain assumptions; empirical residual resampling is not unrestricted distribution-free inference. The April 2004 manuscript studies one-day intervals in GARCH simulations varying persistence and innovation tails. Coverage, width and point-estimation error are different reported criteria, evaluated under those specified laws. Selected FHS/conditioning and simulation passages were read. No uniform finite-sample, every-history or anytime coverage theorem is certified. External foundations, complete numerical results, final-edition identity, implementation and present material permissions remain unverified. No market observations were retrieved.

## Original crosswalk of targets and clocks

| Distinction | Boundary retained |
|---|---|
| Event probability versus continuous tail law | A histogram specifies probabilities of its defined bins. Open tails and changing definitions do not determine support endpoints, within-bin densities or tail means. |
| Issuance versus public availability | Survey response dates, public release dates and later outcome vintages can differ. Reusing a target year across surveys creates distinct forecast horizons with shared settlement outcomes. |
| Future process uncertainty versus current estimation uncertainty | Recursive future scenarios describe possible subsequent values. Risk-estimator intervals describe uncertainty in a conditional functional evaluated at the observed origin. |
| State updates versus parameter refits | Propagating fitted volatility states with fixed coefficients differs from generating pseudo histories and refitting those coefficients. More paths alone do not add the latter uncertainty. |
| Scenario probability versus valuation | A repricing formula maps scenarios into portfolio values; it does not itself identify the scenarios' physical probability law. |
| Numerical evidence versus probability guarantee | Source simulations, terminal percentiles and interval widths retain their specified models/conditioning. They do not automatically yield exact finite-sample, simultaneous or arbitrary-stopping coverage. |

These are source and interpretation distinctions. No new method, target, dataset, experiment or project result is selected.
