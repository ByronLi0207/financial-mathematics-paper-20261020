## 1. Distribution-score sensitivity to observation error

**Onno Kleen (2024), “Scaling and measurement error sensitivity of scoring rules for distribution forecasts.” Journal of Applied Econometrics 39(5), 833–849, August 2024.** First published online **19 April 2024**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.3056); [official body actually selected](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jae.3056).

**Actual body/scope:** selected official publisher HTML reader, with retained reader lines 15–62, 88–241, 245–268, 298–305 and 310–413 covering metadata, definitions, main statements, selected simulation interpretation and the volatility application. These are reader-line coordinates, not printed-page coordinates. No journal PDF was acquired. The retained fingerprint below identifies a selected reader extraction rather than original HTML/PDF bytes. Appendices A–C remain unread after an ordinary supplementary-access failure; external proofs, all graphs/result cells and implementation were not independently certified.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_START -->
Kleen studies unit scaling and the sensitivity of distribution scores to additive measurement error. His sensitivity criterion bounds the expected absolute change caused by perturbing a verification outcome; it does not bound the raw score or its sampling variance. For CRPS, predictive laws with finite first moments and an error class sharing a first-absolute-moment envelope yield a forecast-independent perturbation bound. A sufficiently large true expected-score gap can then preserve an ordering, without universal proxy invariance. The financial application compares SV and HAR predictive distributions for next-day volatility using five-minute and fifteen-minute realized-variance proxies. Both remain measurements rather than observed latent integrated variance. The retrospectively selected stock universe, rolling estimation and fixed-time Diebold–Mariano comparisons qualify the reported ranking stability. This is an established financial distribution-evaluation application, distinct from conditional-mean loss robustness and specified observation-model correction. Selected main text was read; supplementary proofs, filtering implementation, proprietary observations and the replication archive's contents remain unreviewed. No sequential or trading-value guarantee follows from this evidence.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_1_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** retain the common error-moment envelope, score-specific predictive-density restrictions and defined expected comparisons. An individually finite error moment for every member does not supply a common bound. Conditional-mean robustness in Patton's earlier work and Hoga–Dimitriadis addresses a different target; Bessac–Naveau additionally specifies the observation/state model. The source's ordinary CRPS application does not establish a financial application of every scaled or robust CRPS variant.

**Application, access and rights:** the study uses 83 end-2018 S&P100 stocks continuously traded in 2010–2018, rolling 1005-observation estimates and 1258 evaluation days in 2014–2018. NYSE TAQ and CRSP/WRDS inputs are proprietary. The article carries CC BY 4.0; that does not authorize separate observations or programs. The article-linked [replication DOI](https://doi.org/10.15456/jae.2024075.1031781741) was not successfully inspected on the ordinary route. Its archive version, contents and licenses remain unverified; no program or observations were obtained.

## 2. Conditioning a hidden-state score on an imperfect observation

**Julie Bessac and Philippe Naveau (2021), “Forecast score distributions with imperfect observations.” Advances in Statistical Climatology, Meteorology and Oceanography 7, 53–71.** Published **23 September 2021**, following receipt on 15 February, revision on 12 July and acceptance on 28 July 2021. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5194/ascmo-7-53-2021); [formal PDF actually selected](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/7/53/2021/ascmo-7-53-2021.pdf).

**Actual body/scope:** the 19-page formal PDF, selected PDF pp.1, 3–9, 11–13 and 15–16. Reading includes the short Appendix A mean/variance proof, Gaussian posterior/log-score argument in Appendix B, Appendix C's CRPS derivation route and Appendix E's Gamma posterior/log-score argument. PDF pp.4 and 13 were visually inspected. Full figures, all power calculations, external Stein identities, remaining Appendices F–G and numerical reproduction were not independently checked.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_2_START -->
Bessac and Naveau replace an ideal hidden-state score by its conditional expectation given an imperfect observation. Under the specified joint state/observation law and integrability, iterated expectation preserves the ideal score's population mean. Square integrability additionally gives a single-case variance reduction through total variance. This construction requires the posterior state law, or a state prior and observation-error mechanism; arbitrary fitted denoising is not covered automatically. Their Gaussian and Gamma examples integrate posterior uncertainty explicitly. Substituting only the posterior mean generally omits terms required by the corrected score. The wind application uses hourly measurements and numerical-weather predictions under a fitted joint model and an assumed observation-error variance. It is not a financial-volatility validation. The fixed-forecast conditioning argument also does not by itself justify random dynamic forecasts under an enlarged information set. Compared with Kleen's perturbation bound, this method uses stronger model information. Selected formal body and short proofs were read; remaining derivations, external identities, data and executable code were not independently verified.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_2_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** distinguish the finite expected-score condition for the mean identity from the extra second moment for variance reduction. The latter is a single-case result, without automatic long-run covariance reduction for dependent averages. The conditioning law must match the actual forecast/evaluation information. This source's posterior conditioning, Hoga–Dimitriadis's conditional-mean equality and Kleen's moment-envelope sensitivity have different information requirements. No unread original source is promoted to proof-level evidence through this comparison.

**Application, access and rights:** the application concerns 744 transformed hourly surface-wind observations during January 2012. The formal article is CC BY 4.0. It points to an author repository named `uncertainty_scoring`; its present contents, version and code/data licenses were not inspected. No observation or executable package was obtained. Public article access does not settle separate reuse rights.

## 3. Exact conditional comparison robustness and separate test conditions

**Yannick Hoga and Timo Dimitriadis (2023), “On Testing Equal Conditional Predictive Ability Under Measurement Error.” Journal of Business & Economic Statistics 41(2), 364–376, April 2023.** Published online **3 February 2022**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2021.2021923); [actual arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11104v1).

**Actual body/scope:** [arXiv:2106.11104v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11104v1), submitted **21 June 2021, 13:39:22 UTC**, with title page dated 22 June 2021; 56 PDF pages including mathematical appendices. Existing selected reading covers PDF pp.1, 6–11, 13–20, 26–31 and 36–47: definitions, class conditions, affine preservation mechanism, CLT/HAC proof route, covariance decomposition, GDP application and restricted quantile discussion. The formal body and supplement remain unread/uncompared after an ordinary publisher access failure. External characterization/probability proofs, all result cells and later simulation derivations were not independently certified.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_3_START -->
Hoga and Dimitriadis study forecast comparisons when verification outcomes are mismeasured. Exact robustness preserves conditional expected paired loss differences under admissible proxies sharing the target's conditional mean. Their class-based characterization relates this property to conditional-mean targets and Bregman losses; restricted distribution classes retain separate qualifications. The preservation mechanism does not preserve realized scores, variances, p-values or every sample ranking. Their instrumented Wald comparison separately requires dependence rates, higher moments, nondegenerate covariance and bounded forecast memory, with additional conditions for HAC estimation. Proxy quality affects local asymptotic power through the long-run covariance, including cross-time terms. Lower unconditional noise variance alone need not order that covariance. The GDP application compares historical forecasts against release vintages and other ex-post measurements, while admitting that relative proxy checks cannot exclude common bias against latent truth. This differs from whole-law distribution scoring and rate-based latent-target inference. Selected June 2021 preprint body and proof routes were read; the final journal body, external proofs, programs and observations remain unreviewed.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_3_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** both forecasts use the same pre-verification information field. Conditional mean equality is stronger than unconditional unbiasedness or high correlation. Theorem 1 retains its law/action-class, surjectivity, strict-consistency and expectation-domain assumptions. Theorem 2 is an asymptotic fixed-sample-size comparison, with finite-dimensional predictable instruments; it is neither an omnibus test of every conditional alternative nor an optional-stopping result. The precision comparison needs its cross-time covariance qualifications. Patton's earlier imperfect-proxy ranking work is an antecedent; Li–Patton instead permits an asymptotically negligible proxy hypothesis gap without requiring exact conditional unbiasedness.

**Application, access and rights:** SPF/Greenbook mean GDP forecasts are evaluated at one-, two- and four-quarter horizons over 1985Q1–2019Q3. “Latest” means as of 30 September 2020; it is an ex-post evaluation convention, not an original forecast input. The deposited arXiv paper links CC BY 4.0, while formal metadata records different article terms; neither establishes separate program/data rights. The formal supplement is described as including R code, but was not opened or downloaded. Historical vintages, implementation and current reuse permissions were not revalidated.

## 4. Rate-based inference from high-frequency proxies

**Jia Li and Andrew J. Patton (2018), “Asymptotic inference about predictive accuracy using high frequency data.” Journal of Econometrics 203(2), 223–240, April 2018.** The actual journal-formatted body states **available online 31 January 2018**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2017.10.005); [author-hosted article actually selected](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Li_Patton_JoE_2018.pdf).

**Actual body/scope:** selected reading across the 18-page article, PDF pp.1–18, with central assumptions/propositions and pp.3–4 separately recovered after an initial truncated extraction. This does not certify every line, cell or cited proof. The [author-linked 36-page supplement](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Li_Patton_HFPA_june17_appendix.pdf) is explicitly dated **3 June 2017**; selected pp.1–4 cover Propositions 3.1–3.2 and Theorem 4.1, pp.14–16 cover bipower/semivariance proofs of Theorems 4.5–4.6, and pp.22–25 cover StepM/MCS assumptions and proof routes. Supplement p.24 was visually inspected. Exact equivalence to the final publisher supplement, intervening technical arguments, external Jacod–Protter/Protter results and full numerical reproduction remain unverified.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_4_START -->
Li and Patton connect high-frequency proxy approximation to asymptotic inference about latent forecast targets. Their central requirement makes the proxy-versus-latent expected evaluation gap negligible after the comparison test's normalization. Consistency alone supplies no adequate rate, and faster-normalized nested-model tests can require stronger approximation. Sufficient conditions combine proxy moment rates, a stochastic Hölder bound for paired evaluations and dual-moment control. The framework still assumes valid joint convergence, studentization and critical values for the feasible comparison. Primitive results cover distinct semimartingale variation targets under explicit jump, moment, smoothness and sampling conditions. Existing StepM and model-confidence-set extensions retain their additional subset and superior-set assumptions. The financial application forecasts integrated correlations using rolling DCC variants and proprietary intraday data. Proxy negligibility does not repair an underlying comparison test's size distortion. This rate-based route complements exact conditional-mean robustness rather than replacing its assumptions. Selected article and dated supplement proofs were read; external probability results, final-supplement equivalence, programs and observations remain unverified. No finite-sample or sequential guarantee is inferred.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_4_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** the central normalization-scaled hypothesis gap differs from pathwise closeness of two separately constructed test statistics. Retain joint long-span/infill restrictions, target-specific rates, moments and deterministic sampling. Integrated continuous variance, total quadratic variation and signed semivariance are different targets. Paired squared losses can cancel outcome-squared terms; this does not make each individual squared loss globally Lipschitz. MCS results assume the stated timewise superior set and critical-value conditions, rather than an arbitrary changing best model. Hoga–Dimitriadis supplies a distinct exact-moment route; Patton–Sheppard supplies an existing signed-variation financial application with different empirical sampling.

**Application, access and rights:** one-day integrated-correlation forecasts for PG–GE and MSFT–AAPL use TAQ during January 2000–December 2010, 2733 days, rolling 1500-day estimation and 1233 evaluation days. Forecast-input frequency and verification-proxy frequency are distinguished. Neither public author access nor the article's financial application establishes raw-data redistribution rights. No current executable archive, program license or small public replacement dataset was verified; no observations or programs were acquired.

## 5. Signed variation is already a financial forecasting input

**Andrew J. Patton and Kevin Sheppard (2015), “Good Volatility, Bad Volatility: Signed Jumps and the Persistence of Volatility.” The Review of Economics and Statistics 97(3), 683–697, July 2015.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/REST_a_00503); [author-linked journal-layout PDF actually selected](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_Sheppard_REStat_2015.pdf). An exact first-online day was not independently certified; the issue date is not replaced by a metadata-deposit timestamp.

**Actual body/scope:** 15-page journal-layout copy, selected PDF pp.1–6 and 8–15: definitions, sampling, forecasting regressions, inference, signed jumps, international application, pseudo-out-of-sample evaluation, conclusion and references. PDF pp.3 and 12, printed pp.685 and 694, were visually inspected. PDF p.7, the separate online appendix, external theoretical proofs, executable programs and every result cell/curve remain unreviewed. The Li–Patton supplement extension recorded above is not counted as another new source here.

<!-- SYNTHESIS_5_START -->
Patton and Sheppard use signed intraday variation to extend established HAR volatility forecasting. Positive and negative realized semivariances partition realized variance; their limits each contain half the continuous variance and their corresponding signed jump variation. Their difference measures net signed jump variation. Positive and negative parts of that net quantity do not separately recover all one-sided jump variation because opposing contributions can cancel. The application forecasts average future variation over one to sixty-six days, using rolling weighted least squares, QLIKE and fixed-sample comparisons. Its targets are conditional mean variation measures rather than return densities, expected shortfall or drawdown distributions. Overlapping horizons and the correlated stock panel need their own covariance treatment. Business-time transaction sampling in this application differs from Li–Patton's deterministic-sampling theory, so a shared estimator name does not certify all inference conditions. The reported predictive gains vary across assets, horizons and specifications. Selected journal-layout passages were read; the separate appendix, exact implementation, observations, current program availability and reuse rights remain unverified. Trading profits were not established.
<!-- SYNTHESIS_5_END -->

**Conditions and closest work:** finite-sample semivariance is defined by the sign of each intraday return, rather than the day's net return. The source's signed-jump regression also uses bipower variation; the two semivariances alone do not identify the continuous component. Corsi's HAR model, established realized-semivariance theory, Patton's proxy-ranking work and DM/Giacomini–White comparisons are direct antecedents. One-sided variation, a conditional mean and a full conditional law remain different evaluation objects. Changing assets or renaming a downside statistic does not establish a new method.

**Application, access and rights:** the U.S. study uses SPDR and firms ever in the S&P100 during June 1997–July 2008, with at least four years of availability. The 105-firm descriptive/regression sample falls to 95 for the out-of-sample length requirement. It excludes overnight returns, uses approximately five-minute business-time sampling and averages grids. Rolling estimates use 1004 observations; QLIKE positivity includes a training-window forecast floor. The article refers cleaning details to an unread appendix. TAQ and Thomson Reuters Tick History are proprietary. A public article or historical code homepage establishes neither a current paper-specific archive nor rights to redistribute programs or observations. Multi-day label endpoints and initialization were not certified without implementation.
