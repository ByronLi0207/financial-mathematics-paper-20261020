## Original source summary — 145 words

Barrieu and El Karoui study optimal financial risk transfer when counterparties can hedge through different convex opportunity sets. A bounded terminal claim and its price are chosen jointly under the buyer’s participation condition. Inf-convolution represents market-adjusted risk and connects risk sharing with pooled hedging. Explicit sharing rules require a common dilated risk family and the relevant market assumptions; finite values alone do not establish an attained or uniquely decomposable contract. The existence analysis uses bounded opportunity sets, continuity and almost-sure closures. A dynamic entropic example additionally relies on admissible predictable strategies and imported market-duality premises. The paper therefore provides mature antecedents for combining contract design, pricing and unequal hedging access. These are analytical results, not empirical forecast calibration or statistical uncertainty guarantees. This register records selected statements and supplied proof routes in an undated author copy; publisher and accepted-version bodies and external proofs remain unread.

## Mature structure and its assumptions

**Joint contract, price and hedging choice.** The buyer's optimized risk and participation constrain the bounded terminal claim and price. Parties can have different convex sets of bounded terminal gains from zero initial value. Pricing compares optimized positions with and without the claim. This is a model-based indifference or participation value, not an observed quote or profit. Hedging-adjusted risk at zero must remain finite.

**Convexity, cones and decomposition differ.** A cone or vector space adds directional or liquidity structure. Theorems 2.3 and 4.2 assume an attained pooled hedge decomposable into the parties' admissible opportunities. A value identity supplies neither attainment nor decomposition. Proposition 4.1's general dilated common-market simplification requires a cone; the earlier exponential special case cannot replace that premise. Splits may be nonunique. Residual-wealth uniqueness conditional on the pooled optimum is not universal contract or strategy uniqueness.

**Explicit sharing requires a common base criterion.** Same-base dilation supports representative risk tolerance and the semigroup structure. Arbitrary different criteria do not inherit the quota rule. The exponential parameter is risk tolerance, not risk aversion, confidence or a statistical radius. Positive homogeneity makes dilation invariant for a coherent risk measure; changing tolerance need not change preferences. Proposition 3.10's limits were not independently proved.

**Finite values, dual values and primal attainment differ.** Theorem 3.6 retains properness at zero and the associated finite-value condition. Adding penalties gives a dual value representation, not an existence theorem for an optimal primal contract. Acceptance-set sums involve the stated closure operation. The initial framework permits additive dual measures; probability representations, countable additivity and attainment require further continuity or topological premises. Theorem 4.4's common optimal additive dual measure is not a unique physical return distribution. The market-adjusted baseline constant also affects the buyer's indifference price.

**Existence uses bounded sets and closures.** Theorem 5.2 uses convex opportunity sets uniformly bounded in L-infinity, the stated finite lower bound and Section 5 risk continuity. Attained variables lie in almost-sure closures. The supplied route uses Cesaro subsequences and continuity. Closure attainment is not feasibility in the original unclosed strategy set; decomposition separately controls both component sets. No numerical convergence certificate was established.

**The dynamic H1–H4 premises exceed no-arbitrage alone.** Collectively, the inspected entropic example includes locally bounded semimartingale prices, a usual filtration, bounded endowment, an equivalent finite-relative-entropy local-martingale measure, the specified predictable admissible strategy class, and an explicit primal-dual equality assumption. This record does not reconstruct the individual H-number assignments or prove the imported market-duality result. Proposition 5.3's unique optimal probability is conditional on this model. It does not imply universal uniqueness of the financial contract, each trading process or the physical return law.

## Connections and limits of transfer

Payoff-risk decreases with wealth and subtracts added cash. Translation to JST's increasing monetary utility or positive-loss notation requires checking argument, cash and price conventions together. Under Corollary 3.7, folding opportunities into risk does not preserve law invariance merely because the starting criterion has it. The transformed functional needs checking before applying comonotone risk sharing. A restricted insurance-contract class is not automatically stable under the transformation.

Joint contract and price choice, unequal hedging access, particular common-market quota-sharing rules, and dynamic entropic hedging are already mature antecedents here. A different asset panel, an access restriction or a time index alone does not establish methodological novelty. An equivalent pricing measure is not an estimated physical loss law or a confidence ball. The dual representation supplies no statistical calibration of distributional uncertainty. Chronological estimation, financial dependence, costs and inference require their own evidence.
