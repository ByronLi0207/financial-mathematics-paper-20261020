# Mature methods for sampled net-position peak forecasting

The working paper applies established quantile and historical-response constructions to the largest absolute sampled net position over the current and next H records. Its closest same-source predecessor already forecasts signed position endpoints with nearest-neighbor and other conditional regression engines. The substantive application change is the peak response, its known current floor and the completion time of its labels. Those changes create a different comparison; they do not create a new quantile estimator or establish predictive superiority.

The [paper source evidence map](paper-evidence-map.md) identifies the fourteen entries actually cited in the manuscript. This companion compares nine method and evaluation works examined during the revision, including the CAViaR and SPCI routes now cited in the manuscript and seven other neighbors. It records what could be transferred and what would still require a new implementation, an applicable stochastic assumption or independent financial evidence. The 2026 items are recent preprints; no popularity or priority ranking is inferred from their dates.

## The common financial target and information rule

Let N_t be the observed signed source-reported position, B_t=abs(N_t), P_t the maximum abs(N) from record t through t+H, and G_t=P_t-B_t. The response G_t is nonnegative and can equal zero. A forecast is issued after observing record t. A historical origin i has a completed peak label only when i+H is at most t. H measures file records, including the source's long adjacent gaps, rather than elapsed hours.

Any transferred fitted model would therefore need historical features formed with the information available at their own issue origins, completed training and calibration responses, and a declared update schedule. A previously issued forecast is known before its horizon finishes; its final residual or successful nonbreach generally is not. The current rule also does not verify historical public-release timing or a live data vintage.

The existing F rule has the mature residual-quantile form B_t plus a historical quantile of G. S instead reanchors raw observed increment paths at the current signed position. The third evaluated rule, K, reports the larger of B_t and a historical quantile of native peaks P_i. The extension changes membership of historical episodes using one signed starting position, then compares three response constructions. Conditional regression, residual calibration, scenario transformation and historical membership are distinct operations. None can be represented by another operation's name alone.

The implementation boundary is as follows. The [frozen extension protocol](../../protocol/revision-20261021-exploratory-protocol.md) and [accepted results](../../results/revision-20261021/README.md) govern the executed comparison.

Two mature routes can supply future comparisons. A direct conditional model can estimate an upper quantile from completed peak or headroom labels using declared features known at issue. A conditional path route can instead model the joint conditional law of future positions or paired increment extrema and derive the maximum's quantile, without needing to fit peak labels directly. Separate marginal distributions generally do not identify that quantile. Either comparison needs compatible issue timing, common evaluation origins and quantile preference, the known floor and the better original references. These are possible designs, not fitted results.

For known issue position x and a candidate level c≥abs(x), let a and b be the minimum and maximum changes N_(t+h)−x over 0≤h≤H, so a≤0≤b. The event {P_t≤c} is exactly {b≤c−x and −a≤c+x}. A joint conditional law of that envelope pair can therefore be sufficient; a complete generative law for every path coordinate is not universally required. This target identity neither estimates that law nor validates pooling historical envelopes.

Response completion follows the estimator's actual response. A one-record dynamics model may use already observed one-record transitions; they need not be delayed by H records solely because the forecast target is an H-record peak. Direct peak/headroom fitting and calibration using H-record peak errors still need their completed H-record outcomes. For example, at H>1 a recursion cannot use G_(t−1) as observed news when issuing at t: that label finishes after t. G_(t−H) has completed under the present convention, while a previously issued forecast can already be known. Replacing the news input defines an adapted specification whose estimation and assumptions would need to be stated; it does not automatically inherit the source model's result.

An unimplemented one-sided calibration illustration fits g on a separate proper training set, then uses that fitted function for both calibration and prediction. From m completed calibration labels, form E_i=G_i−g(X_i). When k=ceil((m+1)τ) is at most m, report C_t=B_t+max(0,g(X_t)+E_(k)). Nonnegative G does not force E to be nonnegative. Projecting the same raw report up to its known floor weakly reduces its pinball loss and removes no previously covered outcome; this compares only that report with its own projection. A rank-based marginal-coverage argument requires calibration/test score exchangeability, including appropriate conditioning on the proper training data and fitting randomness. It averages over calibration and test samples, rather than conditioning on the observed calibration set, query state or full market information; g need not be the true conditional quantile. Chronology alone establishes none of those exchangeability premises here. [CQR supplement, Appendices B–C](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/5103c3584b063c431bd1268e9b5e76fb-Supplemental.zip).

### A finite endpoint in Schmitt's version 3

Schmitt's displayed finite endpoint needs a qualification even with many records. Take any fixed window m≥1, already transformed regime features equal to zero, zero base forecasts, independent historical scores Uniform[0,1] and an independent current score Uniform[2,3]. With age decay λ=log(100), historical weights are 100^(−k) at age k, and W=(1−100^(−m))/99<1/99. At a 95% target, the displayed clipped quantile level is 1: the report is the finite historical maximum, at most 1, whereas the current loss is at least 2. Coverage is zero. The displayed independence and drift assumptions hold with L_z=0 and L_t=1; each historical/current score-law total-variation distance is 1. Yet the displayed gap is 2W/(W+1)<.02, giving a lower coverage bound above .93. Dropping the kernel changes nothing in this construction. This real-arithmetic endpoint counterexample concerns the fixed version's displayed rule and assumptions, not unread code, other versions, its empirical findings or the present inventory forecasts. [Version 3, Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03903v3).

## Historical scenarios and active hedging

Pritsker's institutional working paper distinguishes model responsiveness and historical sampling limitations. Its controlled comparisons provide a useful reporting practice: identify the mechanism being tested, state which parameters are known and retain adverse cases. More simulated paths or repeated use of overlapping labels cannot create missing tail innovations or a second market record. This supports careful model-risk interpretation; it does not supply a forecast, calibrated filter or uncertainty interval for the present source. [FEDS 2001-27 original](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2001/200127/200127pap.pdf).

Grelsson's 2026 note expresses familiar historical shifts through explicit innovation extraction and volatility-scaling assumptions. That representation is relevant to stating exactly what raw reanchoring assumes. Price-relative changes cannot be copied mechanically to signed inventory crossing zero. For the present deterministic peak calculation, paired increment extrema suffice after raw translation; their adequacy as future conditional envelopes is a separate statistical question. A recursive dynamics correction may require ordered paths. The note's one-step formulas do not establish a peak-forecast improvement or a new estimator for this paper. [Fixed version 1, Sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10066v1).

An elementary illustration of the working paper's Appendix A.4 uses the known recursion N_(s+1)=(1/2)N_s+ε_(s+1), with H=2, historical start N_i=0 and query x=1. The artificial historical paths (0,1,0) and (0,0,1) have identical extrema (a,b)=(0,1) of changes measured from their common start. Both raw translated peaks equal 2. Replaying each path's own innovations under that recursion instead gives peaks 3/2 and 5/4, respectively. The stored historical envelope alone therefore cannot generally recover the dynamically replayed peak. This information-loss example fits no inventory law and compares no forecast losses.

Al-Baghdadi and coauthors combine active FX hedge fractions and evaluate profit and drawdown. Their client, hedge and net exposures are distinct from this passive peak response. The basic expert bound requires a convex decision set and convex losses, advice available before the learner reports and a uniform expert-loss spread. This need not bound each absolute USD loss. For pinball, report diameter can control loss spread; the floor alone cannot. Aggregation is unrun here. [Protocol 1 and Theorem 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v179/al-baghdadi22a/al-baghdadi22a.pdf). [Author-repository metadata](https://github.com/Najim-Al/PhD_AA/tree/a74afef5531eff55795d08e201738f6c11504835) establishes neither independent dealer provenance, mapping to the accepted scalar nor redistribution permission; observation blobs were not opened.

### An unimplemented aggregation design check

For a future comparison, declare τ∈(0,1), J≥2 experts and D>0 before its outcomes, then clip each issued extra allowance to r_(j,t)=min(D,max(0,C_(j,t)−B_t)). For finite G_t, pinball loss ρ_τ(G_t−r), where ρ_τ(e)=τmax(e,0)+(1−τ)max(−e,0), is convex in r and has report Lipschitz constant c_τ=max(τ,1−τ). The expert-loss spread is therefore at most L=c_τD even without a uniform bound on G_t. Capping defines different reports; no D is selected here.

With uniform initial weights, the mature WAA construction uses w_(j,n) proportional to exp(−η_n sum_(s<n) ℓ_(j,s)), r_n=sum_j w_(j,n)r_(j,n) after weight normalization, and η_n=2sqrt(ln J)/(L sqrt(n)). Here n counts a learner's local forecasts and ℓ is its corresponding issued-report loss. This rescales cumulative losses with the current rate. Under the cited conditions, its instantaneous-feedback cumulative loss is at most the best fixed expert's loss plus L sqrt(n ln J).

Delay handling is mature too: [Joulani, György and Szepesvári (2013), Sections 2.1 and 3.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/joulani13.pdf) describe the separate-instance reduction and retain the base algorithm's conditions. In this paper's convention, origin t−H finishes before the forecast at t. H learner states, used cyclically by t modulo H and updated only with their own completed feedback, therefore suffice. Start each learner with uniform weights and a fresh local clock at its first declared scored use, without weight prewarming. They are not statistically independent samples. For the first M consecutive scored origins from that initialization, after their labels complete, summing the local bounds and applying sum_h sqrt(n_h)≤sqrt(HM) gives an excess-loss bound L sqrt(HM ln J) relative to one fixed capped expert index over that scored prefix.

This unimplemented design illustration uses real arithmetic and mature results; it claims no new theorem. Its comparator consists of the capped reports; it supplies no guarantee against the original uncapped rules, switching oracle, conditional quantile truth, coverage or policy return. It has no fitted parameter, forecast, chosen cap or performance result in this study.

The same distinctions delimit the classic filtered historical-simulation sources already cited in the manuscript. Predicted-scale adjustment changes scenario values. Resampling standardized innovations and recursively generating conditional paths before portfolio revaluation uses a declared dynamic model. Selecting nearby historical starting positions changes scenario membership. The current S rule performs raw reanchoring; a result for S does not test all of filtered historical simulation.

## Forecast comparison and dependence

Giacomini and White evaluate complete forecasting methods, including their estimation windows and information choices. Their framework distinguishes conditional from unconditional expected-loss comparisons. Its multi-step theory requires specified process, moment and covariance conditions; the H-minus-one covariance cutoff associated with the full conditional null is not a generic consequence of overlapping targets. Their unconditional comparison uses a suitable long-run variance estimate with a growing lag truncation.

Using the current state in retrieval is different from conditionally evaluating a loss difference. This study reports fixed paired means, rather than a fitted conditional expected-loss comparison or an evaluated state-contingent rule for switching forecasts. Comparing complete forecasting methods also does not require interpreting their membership differences causally. An isolated proximity channel would be a further question, rather than a prerequisite for the stated finite-record rule comparison.

An analytical example separates overlap from misspecification. Fix τ∈(0,1), let Z_j be IID Exp(1), N_0=0, N_t=sum_(j≤t)Z_j and F_t=σ(Z_1,…,Z_t). With H=2 this increasing path gives P_t=N_t+Z_(t+1)+Z_(t+2). For the unique Gamma(2,1) τ-quantile c_τ, q_t=N_t+c_τ satisfies Pr(P_t≤q_t given F_t)=τ. Adjacent breach indicators I_t=1{P_t>q_t} share V=Z_(t+2). Given V they are independent, with common exceedance probability p(V)=exp(−(c_τ−V)) for V<c_τ and 1 otherwise. Their unconditional covariance is therefore Var(p(V))>0. Correct conditional peak quantiles can have correlated adjacent breaches. This artificial increasing path is not an inventory law, stationary-process example, simulation or verification of the cited process assumptions. It supplies no covariance cutoff for the actual record.

For this paper, the stored paired means describe realized score differences on the common record. Neither a fixed rolling pool nor the existence of finite sample losses establishes the cited asymptotic premises. Spacing origins to remove direct label overlap would not by itself prove independence of the underlying positions or fitted forecasts. The existing four resampling ranges remain descriptive and concern the original F/S comparison; they provide no uncertainty calculation for the added nearest-state contrasts. [Journal article, Sections 2–4 and Theorems 3–4](https://economia.uc3m.es/jgonzalo/teaching/PhdTimeSeries/GiacominiWhite.pdf).

## What the selected comparison establishes

The executed extension compares two complete membership rules at common counts within the same candidate pool. It improves the weaker recent native-peak reference on this inspected record and shows adverse breach, severity and calendar tradeoffs alongside score reductions. It neither isolates a causal effect of state information nor beats the better original full-window F/S rule at each horizon. The [complete results](../../results/revision-20261021/README.md) retain those qualifications and the narrower same-method exception.

The remaining evidence gap is a stronger conditional method fitted to this same peak target and evaluated alongside the existing leading references, plus genuinely independent evaluation information. A price-return series is not an independent dealer-inventory record. An additional model selected after looking at this evaluation interval would remain exploratory on that interval. More citations, finer plots or repeated execution cannot supply either missing comparison or independent validation.
