# Source evidence for the revision draft

This map identifies the editions and actual reading scopes behind the revision's draft references. It extends the original nine-reference map without relabeling selected passages as complete papers. A full text read does not imply empirical reproduction, verification of every external theorem, or inspection of every figure pixel. Repeated visits extend scope; they are not additional distinct papers. No third-party PDF is redistributed.

## Direct source and response lineage

**Wisniewski, Lindsay and Lindsay (2020), Application of Conformal Prediction Interval Estimations to Market Makers' Net Positions.** [Official PMLR record and original](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v128/wisniewski20a.html), volume128, pages285–301. All17 physical pages have now been read in extracted text; selected original pages were also rendered and inspected. This supersedes the narrower selected-page scope in the original map, without claiming all-figure visual QA or reproduction. Sections3.2–3.3 locate the features, forecasting methods and endpoint experiment. Section3.3/physical7 explicitly includes K-nearest neighbors; Table1/physical13 reports KNN quantile/conformal/normalized-conformal variants, and physical14 reports their coverage tests. Nearest-neighbor forecasting on this same financial source is an existing precedent, not introduced by this revision. Section3.5/physical10 averages pinball losses for two signed-position interval endpoints. Those published scores are not numbers for the present absolute peak and one upper-quantile objective. This does not prevent refitting suitable quantile engines on completed peak/headroom labels; that same-target, richer-feature comparison has not been run here.

## Mature method components

**Biau and Patra (2011), Sequential Quantile Prediction of Time Series.** Formal [DOI10.1109/TIT.2011.2104610](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2011.2104610), IEEE57(3),1664–1674. The detailed consulted body is [arXiv0908.2503v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2503v2),31May2010,26 pages, SHA-256 `0eb17c666c19086339e51c5560249c4d5f767e0e1d8435d3e97ad37286bbb24c`. Reading covers definitions, Sections3–4.2, the neighbor/order-statistic construction and selected proof passages, not a certification of every proof. A directed reread of physical7–9 distinguishes the growing, truncated expert aggregation and Theorem3.1's stationary-ergodic, H1–H3 and learning-rate premises from a generic mixing requirement. Its stated long-run average-loss guarantee belongs to that complete strategy. Fixed720/fixed-count retrieval with one signed level and delayed overlapping labels defines a different procedure. The finite construction is mature; the theorem is not inherited.

**Hull and White (1998), Incorporating volatility updating into the historical simulation method for value-at-risk.** [DOI10.21314/jor.1998.001](https://doi.org/10.21314/jor.1998.001), Journal of Risk1(1),5–19. The actual body read is the author's publicly uploaded October1998 manuscript, selected manuscript pages2–10, including forecast-time scale definitions, comparison and capital illustration. The first draft isMarch1998; a platform upload/index date is not the publication year. No claim of final-PDF equivalence or reproduced capital calculation is made. Predicted-scale adjustment changes historical scenario values and is distinct from nearest-state membership or raw increment translation.

**Barone-Adesi, Giannopoulos and Vosper (1999), VaR without correlations for portfolios of derivative securities.** [Consulted USI Quaderno99-04](https://folia.unifr.ch/documents/318043/files/wp9904.pdf), revisedMarch1999,29 physical pages, SHA-256 `787964528e645ecdf2c6bbf494f8dac345e69b2fcfc1a38c1777251708807d30`. **Gneiting (2011), Making and Evaluating Point Forecasts.** [DOI10.1198/jasa.2011.r10138](https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2011.r10138), JASA106(494),746–762. The actual formal17-page journal-layout body has SHA-256 `70a05706f94d82ef1e59435a3c64e25a168815ba9a693c6f3ae2a95a4631c55b`; it is distinguished from the separately visited March2010 arXiv version. Selected functional/decision, weighting, quantile, Theorem9 and proof/discussion passages were read. **Politis and Romano (1994), The Stationary Bootstrap.** [DOI10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870), JASA89(428),1303–1313. The consulted11-page journal scan was visually read on physical2,5–7/printed1304,1307–1309; unusable text extraction did not establish a whole-paper read. The source identifies the circular geometric-block mechanism. Its scalar-mean Theorem2 has specific stochastic-process, moment, dependence, nondegeneracy and block-rate premises; algorithmic stationarity of a resampled series alone does not validate a sampling distribution. Original-study ranges retain their descriptive status because those inferential premises were not established. They concern original full-pool F/S and supply no uncertainty for new nearest-minus-recent contrasts.

**Engle and Manganelli (2004), CAViaR: Conditional Autoregressive Value at Risk by Regression Quantiles.** [DOI10.1198/073500104000000370](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500104000000370), Journal of Business & Economic Statistics22(4),367–381. The original return response, density conditions and one-step feedback are not validated for delayed overlapping peak labels or a headroom atom. No recursive CAViaR model or original experiment was run here.

## Financial mechanisms and path-extreme precedents

**Avellaneda and Stoikov (2008), High-frequency trading in a limit order book.** [DOI10.1080/14697680701381228](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680701381228), Quantitative Finance8(3),217–224. All8 author-hosted journal-layout pages were read in text; SHA-256 `71fa9268339ae82365171caa7416833b3a5d584f05e4f4c087c4ae72d7121a8d`. Sections2.2–2.4 and3.2 supply a stated model in which inventory-sensitive quotes alter fill intensities and future inventory. This is context for distinguishing passive prediction from intervention, not evidence that this sampled client-flow proxy follows the source model or a fitted mean-reverting law.

**Boudoukh, Richardson, Stanton and Whitelaw (2004), MaxVaR: Long-Horizon Value at Risk in a Mark-to-Market Environment.** Journal of Investment Management2(3),14–19; [consulted author manuscript](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rwhitela/papers/maxvar.pdf),9 physical pages, SHA-256 `fbd9912bb0265bfb3fc90f066ad7a48e68d72b57048c85c36de7e93df2339566`. All text was read, with selected original equation/monitoring pages rendered. The author cover is undated; final-layout equality and a DOI are unverified. The target is worst mark-to-market loss relative to initial wealth, rather than running-high drawdown or absolute dealer inventory. Its diffusion calculations are not corrections for unobserved excursions in this record.

**van den Hoogen (2017), Intraday liquidity risk estimation using transaction data: an extreme value theory approach.** [Institutional source](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_3c80dabb-c431-47ac-8622-c8709d473c6f?preview=1), August2017 Delft MSc thesis. Neither its source transactions nor its published figures are same-target benchmarks here.

**Liao and Theodosopoulos (2005), Study on optimal timing of mark-to-market for contingent credit risk control.** [arXivmath/0506077v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0506077v1),4June2005.
