# Proposed v5 contribution paragraphs

These are wording proposals for integration, not claims of global priority. Scientific constants and the two original primary comparisons remain unchanged.

**Contribution 1 — Target and information geometry.** We study the completed maximum of sampled absolute net positions over a future horizon, with the current absolute position as a known lower bound. Relative to the signed endpoint task in the source-data literature, this response requires the complete future label before an observation can enter training. We state the mature-label rule, prediction clock and available information explicitly, and use elementary results to clarify lower-bound projection, the assumptions behind a Lévy-based report, and stability of same-pool, same-rank historical reports. The stability bounds quantify sensitivity to historical starting positions without providing a coverage or forecast-accuracy guarantee. This contribution is a precise, auditable formulation for the sampled-peak task using established mathematical tools.

**Contribution 2 — A target-specific model-validation audit.** We compare established historical and conditional estimators while displaying quantile loss alongside breach frequency, breach severity and report excess. These criteria answer different validation questions, so a lower pinball score can coexist with more breaches without implying a systematic defect in a consistent scoring function. We retain the original primary comparisons, distinguish their matched-history counterparts, and document training maturity, feature lags and refit schedules. Later horizon-coherence, scoring-sensitivity and added-baseline analyses remain post-result/exploratory, with conclusions limited to the inspected records and working reference distributions. The contribution lies in this task-specific evidence and its transparent controls; joint scoring and calibration assessment already have clear precedents in CAViaR and the quantile-evaluation literature.

**Contribution 3 — Bounded gains and documented failures across tasks.** The chronological FX replay supplies score evidence for long-history quantile regression and nearest-neighbor forecasts under the reported working DM and multiplicity calculations. The original FX quantile-regression primary comparison reduces loss by 12.66%, while its matched-history comparison reduces loss by 14.77%; the two comparisons have different reference rules and retain their separate roles. The Treasury primary comparison instead increases loss by 50.79% and raises breaches from 28 to 54, showing that conditional estimation does not provide a general improvement across these tasks. The expanded aggregate panel, preference analysis and additional mature-method baselines retain their unfavorable results and numerical or feasibility failures. These results support a limited model-validation contribution, with compatible unseen-dealer validation and prospective evaluation remaining open.
