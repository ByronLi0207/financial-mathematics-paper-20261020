# Main-figure captions prepared at WP3

## Unified history figure

The unified display retains the 380 earlier provenance records and adds all
102 CFTC conditional-versus-historical comparisons. Negative changes favor
regression, with each point using its own matched historical benchmark.
Complete-range panels retain every outlier; the lower panels repeat those same
records over an explicitly labelled detail range. Eight FX learning-curve
records reuse an earlier period and are not independent observations. The CFTC
legend denotes a separate reporting system; its markets aggregate dealers and
can share shocks. The shorter CFTC histories were already examined, while the
longest history was registered before its forecasts. Shading and dashed lines
summarize the original simulation crossings, conditional on the chosen grid and
coordinate. They do not identify a universally sufficient financial history or
account for selecting the coordinate. The new CFTC check agrees with the
short-history disadvantage counts but fails the registered long-history and
strict-improvement predictions. **History alone does not guarantee a gain.**

## Decision diagram

The diagram combines the registered CFTC history counts, the existing
expanded-menu simulation comparison and the stated CFTC cost-grid range. The
simulation median refers to the full regression at the shorter history in the
constant-parameter experiments and the tested menu; it does not imply that every increase in a
penalty improves performance. Cost fractions compare the full CFTC regression
with its matched historical rules at the two original windows. Reserve and
breach charges vary on a fixed grid, with the exceedance charge held fixed.
The diagram is guidance for evaluating candidates, not an evaluated replacement
policy or evidence of realized savings. **History, shrinkage and stated costs
must be assessed together in the intended application.**
