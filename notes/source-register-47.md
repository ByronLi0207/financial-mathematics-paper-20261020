## Mature framework and guarantee strength

**Contract class.** A bounded, one-period pool has nonnegative initial losses. Fixed ex-ante contributions and residual allocations determine exposure. Admissible residual contracts are comonotone: increasing 1-Lipschitz functions of aggregate loss allocate its layers, alongside zero-sum cash constants. This imposed class does not show that every unrestricted nonconvex-preference optimum is comonotone, or cover arbitrary portfolios/dynamic self-financing trades.

**Uncertainty object.** Each agent has a fixed probability measure and a distortion family. Robustness concerns this family, not a probability-law ambiguity ball. Heterogeneous fixed beliefs are distinct from distributional ambiguity. Optimizing over whole curves does not justify choosing distortions independently at every threshold or freely interchanging supremum and integration.

**Optimality.** Proposition 2.6 relates sum-risk minimization and Pareto optimality through translation invariance and feasible cash transfers. Robust **Theorem 3.3(ii) gives a necessary condition**: a layer assignment against one worst tuple is not thereby a sufficient robust solution or numerical certificate. Singleton **Corollary 3.4 gives an if-and-only-if result** under its conditions. Bounded loss, closed/convex distortion families and the specified compactness/topology route require checking before any portfolio transfer.

**Preference examples.** Prelec comparisons restrict tail ranges and common parameters; their layer rules do not cover arbitrary simultaneous heterogeneity. Remark 3.11 supplies numerical crossing examples, not universal analytic ordering. The probabilistic-risk-aversion index describes preference shape, not volatility or statistical coverage.

## What the historical illustration establishes

The source describes California, New York and Texas flood losses as **553 equal-weight monthly states, January 1978–January 2024**, distinguishing updated financial-loss fields from earlier claim-payment proxies. The current OpenFEMA schema, vintage and aggregation recipe remain unverified.

Calculations use **singleton preferences and a common empirical law**, with assumed inverse-S and power parameters, rather than a fitted ambiguity set or heterogeneous-law experiment. An estimate cited from another study does not fit these participants' preferences to these losses. The insurer's ES tail probability is chosen; a smaller value can produce no insurance in the source model.

No chronological holdout, uncertainty calibration, preference-estimation-error analysis or future-performance guarantee was established. Small correlations do not imply independence; bounded scenarios do not prove bounded population losses. No raw observations, code or fitted model were acquired. Source tables and curves were inspected, with no new reproduction.

**Comparison target and adverse evidence.** Centralized average welfare includes **four agents**, versus **three** for P2P. Welfare is reduced modeled risk, not realized return, quoted premium or causal benefit. The base case reports lower P2P average welfare; **Table 3 includes reverse rankings** under other parameters. There is no uniform dominance. Figure 4 sorts states by aggregate loss, **not chronological time**. Cash-shifted/premium-suppressed curves alone establish neither participation benefits nor free profit. Model premiums were not verified as observed tariffs or current institutional rules.
