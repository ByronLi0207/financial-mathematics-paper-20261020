## What can be borrowed, and what cannot be assumed

The confidence-region foundation starts with a correctly specified, fixed parameter in a known compact rectangle, interior true parameter, accurately observed time-homogeneous Markov data, and its stationary ergodicity convention. Differentiability, score stability/moments, identification and positive model information are substantive requirements. Projected score updating, a modified recursive estimator and ellipsoid geometry are distinct components. Its explicit approximated-region definition uses a weakly consistent centering sequence. Region shrinkage and that stated asymptotic target are not recorded as an ordinary finite-sample or simultaneous all-dates guarantee. A new transfer must verify its own exact inferential statement.

The control predecessor distinguishes a fixed worst parameter from a history-dependent adversary choosing successive parameters, then restricts that adversary using the learning state. The physical and learning coordinates share the same innovation. The displayed DPP uses independent innovations, finite actions, continuity and a bounded continuous terminal loss, with external selection premises. A Bellman representation does not calibrate an arbitrary confidence set or validate arbitrary unbounded finance losses. A general value comparison labeled conjecture in the 2017 preprint is not adopted as proved or verified still open today.

The recent published neighbor already treats two risky assets, known covariance/loadings, unknown drifts, continuous learning and exponential utility. Controls are money amounts, with shortselling allowed and transaction costs excluded. Its rectangle width parameter is linked to confidence heuristically in Remark 2.2. The analytic contribution includes a Gaussian stochastic representation and a learning adjustment to a truncated Merton-type term. Setting the ambiguity width to zero retains dynamic learning; it is not the same baseline as a myopic plug-in Merton rule. A central truncated component need not eliminate the learning adjustment or imply an optimal no-trade band with costs.

## Evaluation evidence and limitations

In the discrete-time illustration, a ten-point quantizer and simulated learning-state grids support the numerical solution. Page 16 explicitly uses the true model to construct the grid, while footnote 9 explains that actual market use would need a model estimated from past observations. This is not a chronological historical holdout. Source-reported comparisons have tradeoffs: point-estimate control has high mean wealth and high variability; bank-only robust control has low variability; adaptive robust has a favorable displayed gain-to-loss balance at many horizons, with an explicit exception at horizon 0.1. A plot of inclusion along one path is not a coverage study.

The recent paper's small-uncertainty expansion specializes to zero correlation and has a time/state-dependent local threshold with an integrable remainder. Uniform accuracy across all states, correlations or finite uncertainty widths is not inferred. Its performance figure reports 8000 time steps and 1000 simulations in a specified artificial setting. A wrong-drift policy has high mean wealth but poor reported utility; the known-drift policy has highest reported utility in that example. Mean wealth and optimized utility are different targets. These are the authors' results, not runs of this project or evidence of net historical profitability.

The recent publisher's data statement makes simulated datasets available from the corresponding author; no such dataset was acquired or contact attempted. The article is CC BY 4.0, which does not establish the public availability of unprovided simulation data or executable code. No third-party paper PDF, figure or raw dataset is included in this repository note.

## Implications for the later research design

These sources already cover broad combinations of learning, robust allocation, covariance and hedging adjustments. Changing an asset, dataset, action grid or numerical solver alone would not establish a new method. A defensible transfer needs a financial question that remains distinct from these neighbors, an appropriate information set, a justified uncertainty target and an interpretable decision comparison.

Useful mature practices include analytic state reduction before adding a large numerical model, an oracle clearly labeled as an oracle, separate dynamic-adaptive and myopic baselines, and explicit accounting for which observations construct the policy grid. The recent article also offers a writing pattern: model and assumptions, tractable characterization, economic interpretation, special-case analysis, then numerical evidence. Any later design must fix its chronological split, evaluation target and resource budget before viewing held-out results and retain adverse or null findings. Those design decisions are not yet frozen here.
