## 1. Dynamic duality, ES and stopping

Yifan Jiang, *Duality of causal distributionally robust optimization*. [Versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16556v3), [original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16556v3), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2401.16556. First deposit 29 January 2024; revision 17 August 2026. The record's final-version comment does not establish journal acceptance.

**Additional scope:** Selected PDF pages 14–24: continuous-time control, ES duality, information example, nested stopping and supplied proof routes. The Wiener special case and external foundational proofs were not independently audited.

**Original synthesis:** A dynamic inner calculation retains global ambiguity inputs; it does not renew a hard budget each date or replace static ES by nested ES. Stopping also requires specifying information, because revealing a latent selection can change value without changing the observed path law. Nested distributions retain conditional-law information. Continuous-time results impose strong regularity on a reference SDE and path-dependent equation, while stopping-value comparisons retain topology and continuity premises. Existing option and pricing discussions are direct financial predecessors. These structural representations do not calibrate sampling uncertainty or certify a small-memory numerical solver. All literal arguments, external dependencies, pricing extensions and executable convergence were not independently certified.

## 2. Sensitivity under causal and martingale constraints

Yifan Jiang and Jan Obłój, *Sensitivity of causal distributionally robust optimization*. [Versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17109v2), [original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.17109v2), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2408.17109. First deposit 30 August 2024; revision 27 May 2025. No journal identity is inferred.

**Actual scope:** Selected definitions on PDF pages 5–8; discrete statements and Asian example 11–13 before Section 4.2; supplied discrete proofs 21–23; Lemma 4.21 on 20–21; Proposition 2.4 proof on 29–30. Continuous-time results remain outside this depth.

**Original synthesis:** Increment-based transport costs yield a local first-order sensitivity involving an optional payoff-derivative projection. Maintaining martingale laws further requires a best predictable approximation; the conditional-mean residual simplification is quadratic-specific. Smoothness, growth, reference moments and penalty scaling remain substantive. The generic proof localizes adversaries and requires admissible near-optimal perturbations; the martingale proof also uses a bi-causal approximation. A synthetic Asian-option example already compares a nonparametric sensitivity with a particular parametric perturbation. A local expansion is neither a finite-radius error bound nor a confidence interval for an estimated coefficient. Supplied local routes were read without independently certifying every literal estimate, external foundation or implementation. Changing the contract or dataset alone would not establish a new method.
