 \section{Implications for position monitoring}\label{sec:implications}
-\textbf{Keep a historical benchmark.} Historical headroom is a demanding reference for these sampled position peaks. The weekly results show why a conditional method should be compared with a historical rule on the same completed labels and update schedule before it is used for monitoring.
+\textbf{Benchmark against historical headroom.} Compare conditional reports with historical headroom on the same completed labels and update schedule. Original regression loses all Treasury-bill comparisons and most additional weekly panel cells.
 
-\textbf{Default to conservative historical reports in short samples.} R6 shows that adding ordinary intercept-only $I$ to the menu and applying one-SE selection still permits large weekly losses: R2 selects $I$ in \RWritingISelected{}/\RWritingWeeklyCells{} cells, yet its worst excess over $F$ is \RWeeklyTwoWorst{}\%. For short samples of this kind, use the historical rule by default and switch to a conditional model only when validation evidence is sufficiently strong; this study establishes no such sufficiency threshold. Prefer conservative corrected-rank $F$ at short windows: earlier-period $I$ itself loses by as much as \RWeeklyIWorst{}\% relative to $F$. An intercept-only option and a larger penalty do not establish evaluation protection.
+\textbf{Default to corrected-rank headroom in short weekly samples.} Use the corrected-rank historical rule as the starting point for these short samples. Wider penalties and conservative one-standard-error selection leave large losses, while the ordinary empirical quantile's excess loss reaches \RWeeklyIWorst{}\%.
 
-\textbf{Report error amounts and breach occasions together.} Mean underprediction and overprediction describe the size of opposite forecast errors, whereas strict breaches count their occurrence. A monitoring report should display all three alongside the stipulated score, since a lower score alone does not establish fewer limit exceedances or lower observed funding cost.
+\textbf{Report breaches with the score.} Show underprediction amounts, overprediction amounts and breach counts alongside pinball loss. The main FX gain accompanies more breaches, so a lower score alone misses part of the monitoring outcome.
 
 \section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
-\textbf{Inspection and inference.} The FX evaluation period had been inspected before the conditional methods were specified, so it is not a clean untouched test. Later statistical inference and the specification sequence describe that same archive. HAC regularity, fixed-window predictive-ability premises and the effective sample information behind overlapping labels require care; a nonoverlap grid does not make the observations independent.
-
-\textbf{Measurement and information timing.} Treasury is a downloaded-vintage replay indexed by economic date, not a reconstruction of historical public releases. The aggregate can hide offsetting dealer positions and includes valuation and composition effects. Both sources sample a position process and omit intrarecord excursions; the FX client-flow record is not a complete hedged dealer book.
-
-\textbf{Coverage and specification.} These procedures have no established conditional-coverage guarantee for these records. Floor atoms distinguish an exact nominal-exceedance null from the upper-bound implication of a quantile, while Treasury's sparse grids make likelihood-ratio approximations fragile. Stronger Treasury shrinkage brings scores closer to the intercept-only reference while leaving more breaches than $F$ in each original Treasury cell. The additional panel retains substantial adverse cells, and the short-history longer-horizon FX failure survives menu expansion. Matching the menu removes one specification difference without identifying a universal or causal value of extra predictors.
+The two data sources describe different dealer-position records. The FX client-flow series samples part of one dealer's activity, while Treasury aggregates positions across reporting dealers. Both omit movements between recorded observations. Treasury observations use a downloaded vintage indexed by economic date, and aggregation can hide offsetting positions and valuation effects.
 
-\textbf{Decision value.} We have no observations of the trading, hedging, collateral or funding decisions needed to measure economic benefit. A lower stipulated score therefore does not establish lower realized financing cost. Active inventory-sensitive decisions would also alter the position process that a passive forecast observes.
+The comparisons describe forecasting performance within these records. The original FX evaluation period was inspected before the conditional methods were specified. The procedure comparisons are not causal and do not identify the effects of individual predictors. Shared reporting and overlapping positions also connect the additional weekly series.
 
-The empirical object is a measured position magnitude; its connection to a particular inventory policy requires the corresponding decision setting.
+Forecast scores and breach counts answer different monitoring questions. Their interpretation depends on the response, rank convention and chosen score. The study provides no conditional-coverage guarantee and measures no trading, collateral or funding benefit.
 
 \section{Conclusion}
-Three findings qualify conditional forecasts of two-sided absolute holding-period peaks. History- and update-matched six-step regression lowers FX loss by \FXMatchedGain{}\% but raises Treasury loss by \TreasuryOriginalExcess{}\%. In the earlier 2015 FX block with no prior evaluation role found in the repository audit, QR lowers loss by \RWritingMatchedGain{}\% against matched frozen $F_{2880}$ but raises it by \RWritingOriginalExcess{}\% against the original primary's rolling $F_{720}$, whose gain direction does not recur; the Treasury $W=52$ analogue raises loss by \RWritingTreasuryExcess{}\%. Expanded ridge menus reduce the \PanelSeriesCount{}-series weekly panel's median excess from \PanelOriginalMedian{}\% to \PanelExpandedMedian{}\%, but \PanelExpandedLosses{}/\PanelCellCount{} cells still lose. Shrinkage mitigates losses, yet short-sample weekly validation selection, including conservative one-SE selection, does not stably protect the historical benchmark. Finally, the original FX primary's \FXPrimaryGain{}\% score gain accompanies breaches rising from \FXReferenceBreaches{} to \FXPrimaryBreaches{}; the \FXLegacyGain{}\% 24-step legacy gain reverses above weight ratio \FXLegacyCrossing{}, and both primary Murphy curves cross. Inventory-limit selection therefore requires underprediction amounts, overprediction amounts and breach counts alongside pinball loss.
+Simple historical rules are hard to beat when forecasting peaks of dealer net positions. In hourly FX, regression improves on matched historical headroom in both evaluation periods (\FXMatchedGain{}\% and \RWritingMatchedGain{}\%). Its gain over the short-history rolling rule appears only in the original period; earlier loss increases \RWritingOriginalExcess{}\%.
 
-These retrospective comparisons apply established estimators to a sampled peak. Prior FX inspection, downloaded-vintage Treasury aggregates, post-result menu expansion and shared panel reporting limit generalization. They establish neither conditional-coverage guarantees nor benefits from actual inventory or funding decisions.
+On weekly positions, stronger regularization narrows regression's gap, but \PanelExpandedLosses{} of \PanelCellCount{} panel cells still lose. Conservative one-standard-error selection also leaves large weekly losses. Lower pinball loss and fewer breaches diverge in the main FX comparison, and both main Murphy curves cross. Position-limit model selection should therefore compare historical rules and report both error amounts and breaches. These retrospective findings from two sources provide no conditional-coverage guarantee and measure no trading or funding benefit.
