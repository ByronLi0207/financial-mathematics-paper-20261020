# WP6 internal review A — Journal of Forecasting associate-editor perspective

This is a fresh internal editorial review, not a review by the journal or an assessment commissioned by its editors. Review baseline: `revision-v8-goal` at `831d5fc763dbf29eb12ce293c46803b96bb470fb` (WP5 completion). Review basis: the current `submission-jof/manuscript.tex`, `supplement.tex` and `cover-letter.md`, together with the accepted WP3–WP5 integration dossier and its acceptance/mismatch records. WP3–WP5 have **not yet been integrated into these TeX sources**; the findings below distinguish current wording from requirements for the forthcoming WP7 integration. Procedural acceptance of a work package is not scientific support for its hypothesis. This review requests writing and presentation changes using existing accepted results only; it requests no new model, data, calculation, test, tuning or external review.

1. **MAJOR — The simulation cannot be sold as an explanation that validates the proposed narrow mechanism.**

   **Evidence.** The current manuscript motivates an estimation-versus-conditioning explanation at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:24` and presents the empirical concentration of gains in C2 at line 42. The not-yet-integrated simulation explicitly reports **MISMATCH**: `results/revision-v8/wp3/README.md:3–21` records 2/12 clear gains inside and 6/60 outside the predeclared region for **each** regression procedure, with outside gains including homoskedastic processes and the longer horizon. `notes/revision-v8/wp3-scientific-mismatch.json` fields `procedures.QR33.status`, `procedures.QR4.status`, `target_region` and `required_narrative` bind that conclusion. `notes/revision-v8/wp3-acceptance.json` separates `procedural_acceptance: PASS` from `scientific_classification: MISMATCH`.

   **Editorial concern.** The experiment adds informative counterevidence, but it does not establish that the original empirical pattern follows from the proposed region of history, horizon and volatility clustering. Presenting it as successful mechanism confirmation would invert the frozen result and weaken the credibility of the entire application.

   **Feasible response.** In the abstract's new sentence, C2/C3, the simulation paragraph, its main heatmap caption and the conclusion, state that the controlled study rejects the predeclared restriction. Describe small-history losses and the observed long-history gains as patterns in the executed grid, without proposing a replacement boundary after inspecting it. Retain all outside-region gains, Monte Carlo uncertainty and the original descriptive support screen in the Supplement. An accurate short formulation is: “The simulation does not confine conditional gains to the predeclared region; the empirical concentration is not a universal mechanism boundary.”

2. **MAJOR — The daily contrast narrows a source-only story but does not separate a pure frequency effect.**

   **Evidence.** The current data discussion acknowledges different measurement and frequencies at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:127`; the decision-map caption already disclaims a causal frequency effect at line 303. Accepted daily evidence gives historical headroom 7/8 score wins, while preserving the four-feature W260/H6 exception: `results/revision-v8/wp4/README.md:3–11`; the latter lowers loss by about 3.0% but records 37 rather than 30 breaches (`results/revision-v8/wp4/COMPLETE_RESULTS.md:13–15`). `results/revision-v8/wp4/README.md:30–37` says physical histories, horizons, update cadence, predictors and sampled targets change jointly. `notes/revision-v8/wp4-acceptance.json` explicitly records `causal_frequency_identified: false`.

   **Editorial concern.** The same recorded dealer producing a largely historical-rule-favoring daily pattern is useful evidence against source identity alone explaining all rankings. It cannot establish that frequency causes the contrast, nor justify a cover-letter claim that frequency and source have been fully separated.

   **Feasible response.** Add the daily row and its exception to the existing descriptive decision map. Call WP4 a “within-dealer daily-aggregation contrast” and explain the joint changes once in the main text. Keep the existing noncausal caption and carry the same boundary into the abstract, C2/C3, conclusion and cover letter. Use “reduces the source-frequency confounding through a same-dealer contrast” only with the explicit remaining confounds; preferably use the more literal description of what was done. Do not convert the frozen `TREASURY_LIKE` label into a causal or general transport claim.

3. **MAJOR — WP5 cannot increase the number of independent validation periods to three.**

   **Evidence.** Current C3 describes two periods at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:44`, and the limitations correctly identify the familiar-source restriction at line 319. The accepted extension states that the full prefix contains all 1,440 earlier backcast origins and that prior R3 H24 scoring already covered its target paths: `results/revision-v8/wp5/THREE_WINDOWS.md:1–3`. `notes/revision-v8/wp5-mismatches.json`, key `MISMATCH-WP5-01`, records the false training-only/never-scored premise. `notes/revision-v8/wp5-acceptance.json` records `fresh_independent_third_period: FAIL_MISMATCH`, `overlapping_old_retro_origins: 1440`, and `scientific_descriptive_screen: SUPPORT_WITH_LIMITS`.

   **Editorial concern.** Three named windows can improve description of this one record, but overlap, prior inspection and later parameter choice prevent interpreting them as three fresh replications or a new independently held-out period. A changed number of table blocks is not stronger validation of that kind.

   **Feasible response.** Name the three windows explicitly and title their display “overlapping/inspected evaluation windows.” State the nesting and later parameter choice in the display note and in the main evaluation design. Update C3 to describe an extended within-record stability diagnostic, rather than “three independent periods.” Preserve old unavailable cells as NA; `results/revision-v8/wp5/README.md:5–9` explicitly prohibits substituting the later five-menu rule for original QR33 or filling missing old tests. Retain the present external/prospective-validation limitation.

4. **MAJOR — The elementary floor argument and classical bound should support the application, rather than carry its novelty case.**

   **Evidence.** C1 leads the contribution list and says “We establish” the floor projection and strict-tail statement at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:40`; the propositions appear at lines 97–111. The Supplement itself correctly calls the floor projection “an elementary pointwise improvement” (`submission-jof/supplement.tex:76–79`) and the endpoint bound “a direct application of the classical Lévy inequality, not a new maximal inequality” (lines 81–93). The true-quantile population claim does not automatically transfer to a fitted empirical report (lines 106–113).

   **Editorial concern.** Neither the known lower support constraint nor the application of the classical maximal inequality supplies a substantial methodological novelty claim on the evidence reviewed. Overweighting these propositions would invite an avoidable priority objection and obscure the more defensible comparative forecasting contribution.

   **Feasible response.** Describe C1 as precise target formulation and implementation consequences: sampled path label, known floor, mature-label timing and horizon coherence. Call the floor result elementary and the bound classical at their first contribution-level mention. Keep the compact propositions and the proofs, but move the empirical question and its benchmark-sensitive answer ahead of them in the contribution narrative. Preserve the distinction between a population bound and the empirically evaluated endpoint plug-in in the cover letter as well.

5. **MAJOR — The nearest literature already evaluates scores and coverage jointly; the incremental claim must be target- and comparison-specific.**

   **Evidence.** The manuscript correctly acknowledges the endpoint lineage's joint scores and coverage tests at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:54`, while C3 opens “We show why peak-limit validation must compare scores, breaches and stability” at line 44. The existing directed literature record identifies Wisniewski's ordered training/calibration/evaluation and joint scoring/coverage as precedents (`notes/revision-v5-novelty/literature-a.md:15–18`). The second literature record also identifies CAViaR hit diagnostics and Ehm et al.'s Murphy comparisons as prior tools (`notes/revision-v5-novelty/literature-b.md:7–13`). The manuscript's literature-table note already cautions that the payment-thesis reading is partial and that `n.r.` is not absence (`submission-jof/manuscript.tex:64–76`).

   **Editorial concern.** “Scores plus breaches,” chronological evaluation, known starting information, path risk and Murphy diagrams cannot individually be claimed as the innovation. A matrix populated with `n.r.` also cannot establish priority for their conjunction across the literature.

   **Feasible response.** Preserve the nearest endpoint/path-risk comparisons and the table's limited-reading note. State the added empirical object directly: same-target peak forecasts with matched mature histories, named rolling controls, and documented failures of score, breach and ranking stability in the observed records. Recast C3 as an application and finding about those comparisons using established evaluation tools. Avoid “first,” absence claims or a new-method claim. No additional literature search is required to make this narrower response.

6. **MAJOR — The Journal of Forecasting case needs one substantive forecasting question and answer, beyond a careful validation checklist.**

   **Evidence.** The abstract combines the FX gains, weekly losses, two mathematical statements and a general joint-validation conclusion (`submission-jof/manuscript.tex:17`). C1–C3 divide attention across formalization, empirical comparisons and diagnostics (lines 40–44). The practical section ends with a general matched-validation process (lines 293–299), and the cover letter calls the contribution a combination of the target, bound and validation evidence (`submission-jof/cover-letter.md:5–7`). The accepted extension adds a specific informative contrast: H6 QR33 full-prefix loss falls 12.2% against matched F2880, with 416 versus 282 breaches; its rolling comparison is materially smaller and its raw descriptive p-value is about 0.055 (`results/revision-v8/wp5/THREE_WINDOWS.md:7–8`).

   **Editorial concern.** Good transparency is essential, but an editor still needs to identify the new forecasting knowledge. The existing evidence supports a bounded application about what extra conditioning buys after benchmark history is matched, and why that answer changes under another legitimate reference. It does not yet support a general mechanism, causal frequency account or externally validated model-selection rule.

   **Feasible response.** Lead the introduction and cover letter with a single question such as whether conditioning improves sampled peak-position forecasts once historical information is matched, and whether any gain survives a changed reference and breach assessment. Give the empirical answer before enumerating the methods: gains exist in named FX comparisons, historical rules dominate many related comparisons, and score gains can buy more breaches or disappear under a different history rule. Use WP3's falsification and WP4/WP5's limitations to delimit that answer. Keep the checklist as the implication of this specific evidence rather than the principal novelty claim.

7. **MINOR — The “short-horizon” headline and decision map need explicit counterexamples once the accepted results are integrated.**

   **Evidence.** Current wording identifies the “clearest repeated” short-horizon gain at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:34`, 42 and 332; that qualified wording need not be discarded. However, `results/revision-v8/wp5/FULL_BACKCAST_RESULTS.md:11–14` reports H24 matched score gains of about 17.5% and 21.0% for QR33 and QR4, with 470 and 355 breaches versus 275 for matched F. H24 QR33 instead loses to rolling F720. `notes/revision-v8/wp5-mismatches.json`, key `MISMATCH-WP5-02`, rejects a literal short-horizon-only claim. The daily QR4 exception is independently retained in `notes/revision-v8/wp4-mismatches.json`, id `MISMATCH-WP4-03`. The current longer-horizon decision-map row is at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:308`.

   **Editorial concern.** An economical headline can remain, but an “only short horizons” or “only hourly observations” reading would conflict with the new dossier. Conversely, secondary H24 gains should not be used to turn the failed primary mechanism screen into support.

   **Feasible response.** Retain “clearest repeated original matched H6 gain” with its named scope. Add the daily exception and the extended H24 matched-versus-rolling contrast to the map or its adjacent paragraph. Keep the full H6/H24 comparison table in the Supplement. Explain that the larger backcast is an inspected diagnostic, not a replacement primary or a rescued mechanism claim.

8. **MINOR — Carry the measurement boundary into every practical interpretation of the daily and aggregate results.**

   **Evidence.** The target is explicitly a sampled maximum at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:83–90`, and the current limitations distinguish aggregate netting, unsampled excursions and actual decision costs at lines 322–325. The accepted daily series consists of 930 nonempty source-naive calendar days under a last-record rule, not a reconstructed financial close or intraday maximum (`results/revision-v8/wp4/README.md:15–22`). Short-history historical F itself has substantial breach frequencies (`results/revision-v8/wp4/COMPLETE_RESULTS.md:7–12`).

   **Editorial concern.** Historical score superiority does not make the historical report a coverage certificate, and a peak over daily last records does not measure the original intraday peak. Weekly signed aggregates likewise cannot be treated as an individual dealer's gross exposure or settlement cash need. These distinctions determine what practical claims the application can sustain.

   **Feasible response.** Define the daily observation clock and sampled target beside its first result; retain “sampled” in the abstract and conclusions. Phrase the decision-map action as retaining historical and parsimonious competitors for joint validation, rather than recommending immediate operational deployment of a winning score. State that asymmetric error preferences are evaluation weights, with actual economic value still unmeasured. The existing caveats can be reused and shortened; no decision simulation or new coverage test is needed.

9. **MINOR — WP7 must replace stale version/provenance statements and distinguish complete records from independent reproduction.**

   **Evidence.** The present Supplement says it accompanies v7 and adds no models, data, tests or selection (`submission-jof/supplement.tex:15–20`), while the main code-availability paragraph says “This revision adds no new financial experiment” (`submission-jof/manuscript.tex:348`). Those are current pre-integration statements, not descriptions of the accepted v8 additions. WP3 explicitly says its independent scalar/provenance audit is not independent study replication (`results/revision-v8/wp3/README.md:37–39`); its release-specific reproduction instructions remain to be finalized (lines 58–62). WP4 and WP5 acceptance files explicitly identify `paper_integration_stage: WP7`.

   **Editorial concern.** Once WP3–WP5 are added, retaining the v7 statements would create avoidable contradictions about what was executed. A complete archive, arithmetic readback and reproducible document source also do not prove independently replicated scientific findings. The cover letter should report the achieved layer accurately.

   **Feasible response.** Update version names and contribution/provenance text together in the main paper, Supplement and cover letter. Describe the three frozen executed additions and their scientific classifications, while preserving original primary results and old archives. Point current-result references to the actual v8 release only after its publication is verified; keep historical links explicitly labelled. Preserve author-completion placeholders. Avoid any claim of independent replication, actual journal review, submission readiness or prospective validation based solely on local acceptance receipts.

**Objective publication boundary.** With these writing and integration responses, the dossier can support an assessable, bounded comparative forecasting/application paper: a precise sampled peak target, demanding historical controls, selected within-record score gains, and informative failures of portability, benchmark stability and score–breach agreement. It cannot support a new maximal inequality, a universally identified narrow mechanism, a pure causal frequency effect, three independent validation periods, independent dealer/prospective validation, or demonstrated operating value. Whether the remaining empirical contribution clears Journal of Forecasting's editorial threshold remains an external editorial judgment. This internal review is not a favorable publication promise or a journal decision. Review A stops here; no manuscript, code or result changes have been made.
