# WP6 internal review B — econometrics

Reviewer role: fresh internal econometric reviewer; this is not an actual journal review or external validation. Reviewed at the WP0–WP5 acceptance boundary, before WP7 integrates the new results into TeX. The submitted review scope is both (i) the current `submission-jof/manuscript.tex` and `supplement.tex`, which still describe the preceding manuscript, and (ii) the frozen WP3–WP5 designs, complete public results, readouts and independent arithmetic/provenance audits. An integration requirement below is not an allegation that an unfinished WP7 has already failed.

The principal problem is inferential scope, rather than a demonstrated label-timing or arithmetic defect. Mature practice here is to define the estimand and information clock, preserve the frozen simulation screen and full failure inventory, quantify Monte Carlo error at the repetition level, distinguish overlapping retrospective diagnostics from new validation, and keep raw exploratory tests separate from their historical adjustment families. The frozen designs already implement many of these safeguards; WP7 must carry them into reader-facing claims. No new forecasts, financial calculations, tests, models, data or tuning are requested by this review. All nine responses below are feasible through writing or existing outputs.

   **Why it matters.** Maturity prevents unfinished peaks from entering a particular fit. It does not establish that the entire forecasting procedure was available at the historical backcast issue time. A reader applying the main evaluation paragraph to WP5 could wrongly infer a fully real-time out-of-sample experiment, even though no label leakage within its fits has been demonstrated.

   **Feasible response and residual limit.** Retain the mature-label equation and add a short explicit WP5 exception beside the evaluation clock: coefficients and transforms use only labels mature at each refit, whereas fixed penalties were chosen later. Call the exercise an inspected, fixed-parameter pseudo-out-of-sample backcast. Put the four penalty identities in the Supplement. The existing audit resolves the recorded maturity/parameter implementation check; writing resolves the misleading generalization. Historical real-time availability cannot be repaired with these results and must remain a limitation.

3. **MAJOR — Make the simulation uncertainty and its design-conditional scope visible outside the archive.**

   **Evidence.** The process uses a 20,000-observation burn-in approximation, not exact stationary initialization, and one specified alpha/beta path for each persistence sum (`protocol/revision-v8/wp3-mechanism-design.md:19–41`). Validation and evaluation contain only 72 issues and three refit blocks each (`:89–98`). There are 32 distinct repetition streams; common random numbers are reused across all conditions within a repetition (`:102–115`). The ratio-of-means delta MCSE, between-repetition breach MCSE, win MCSE, and worst-case win bound are defined at `:128–148`. The screen is explicitly descriptive and not family-wise adjusted (`:152–168`). The public figure captions already explain estimates, MCSE and completed/planned denominators (`results/revision-v8/wp3/figures/captions.md:5,13`).

   **Why it matters.** The independent repetition, not each overlapping issue or each grid cell, is the Monte Carlo uncertainty unit. Cross-cell similarities are correlated because of common random numbers. The worst-case win-frequency MCSE is about 0.0884, so 32 repetitions do not sharply locate a regime boundary. MCSE describes simulation sampling error under this stationary-approximation DGP and short evaluation design; it is not uncertainty about the real dealer population or empirical frequency effects.

   **Feasible response and residual limit.** In the mechanism paragraph/caption state “32 repetitions, common innovations across conditions, 72 evaluation issues per repetition”; retain uncapped estimates with their MCSE and call the screen descriptive. In the Supplement preserve the ratio-of-means definition, the worst-case win bound and initialization qualification. Describe the experiment as a controlled procedure comparison for mean-reverting levels with specified GARCH innovations, not a fitted reproduction of either data source. Existing reporting resolves ambiguity about the uncertainty unit. Limited precision, approximate stationarity, short segments and DGP coverage can only be bounded in prose under the current computation restriction.

The current forecasting-method paragraph is careful about distinguishing regularization and feature changes (`submission-jof/manuscript.tex:140`), but WP7 needs to extend that care to the new simulation.

   **Why it matters.** Freezing each extension before its new output protects against changing that extension after observing it; it does not make the earlier motivation, chosen DGP, menu, or presentation blind to all previous results. A better QR4 outcome could arise from its penalty/selection contract as well as fewer predictors.    **Feasible response and residual limit.** Extend the existing transparency paragraph to name WP3–WP5 as frozen-before-their-own-calculation but post-result additions. Writing resolves chronology and attribution errors. Selection-adjusted confirmation and a pure feature-count identification remain unavailable.

5. **MAJOR — Keep the eight new raw normal-HAC contrasts separate from the original 72-contrast Holm family and from confirmatory significance claims.**

   **Evidence.** `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:262–267` identifies the old two-primary Bonferroni and 72-contrast matched Holm results, while already distinguishing writing-stage earlier-period HAC. The new WP5 contract permits exactly eight normal two-sided DM-type contrasts, Bartlett covariance with divisor n, bandwidth `min(n−1,max(H,floor(4(n/100)^(2/9))))`, nonpositive-LRV/constant-difference NA, and **raw unadjusted descriptive** p-values (`protocol/revision-v8/wp5-fixed-prefix-design.json:111–121`). `results/revision-v8/wp5/FULL_BACKCAST_RESULTS.md:7–14` preserves every new contrast, including H6 QR33/rolling p=0.05505865409429612 and H24 QR33/matched p=0.04587411834032745. `notes/revision-v8/wp5-acceptance.json:11–14,101–105` confirms the eight-comparison scope and absence of new HLN/coverage/family calculations.

   **Why it matters.** A reader must not interpret a raw p below 0.05 as having inherited the old Holm correction, and must not infer equivalence or no effect from a raw p above 0.05. Overlapping labels, online refits, stationarity/regularity assumptions and the full research-selection history limit the nominal HAC reference. A bandwidth at least H accounts for the chosen overlap convention; it does not prove that all dependence has disappeared.

   **Feasible response and residual limit.** Place the eight raw values in a separately labelled WP5 table, with the exact HAC convention and “post-result descriptive; no multiplicity or selection adjustment” in its note. Keep old missing DM/HLN cells NA. Use mean-loss direction, rather than a significance cutoff, for the frozen H6 descriptive screen. Preserve all old primary and Holm fields unchanged. Clear labeling resolves family confusion. Existing results cannot provide full selection control or establish the HAC assumptions, and no new correction or test is requested.

6. **MAJOR — Three displayed windows are not a fresh third validation period: disclose both nested origins and earlier R3 target exposure.**

   **Evidence.** The new prefix is 3071–10438, while the old backcast is 8999–10438, making all 1,440 old backcast origins nested (`protocol/revision-v8/wp5-fixed-prefix-design.md:7–13`; `notes/revision-v8/wp5-independent-audit.json:39–57`).    **Why it matters.** Counting the prefix and its nested subwindow as two independent replications inflates apparent stability.    **Feasible response and residual limit.** Use “three overlapping/inspected evaluation windows,” give both inclusive ranges, and state the 1,440-origin containment plus prior R3 target exposure in the Supplement. Report the three existing H6 matched changes and changing rolling-reference signs as conditional descriptions. Do not pool window effects, combine p-values, count independent successes, or select apparent gaps. The accepted dossier fully resolves the factual exposure classification; a fresh independent period is factually absent and cannot be supplied by wording.

7. **MAJOR — Interpret daily FX as a within-dealer aggregation contrast, with the parsimonious counterexample retained; it does not identify a pure frequency effect.**

   **Evidence.** The frozen rule selects the last record of each nonempty source-naive day and waits until day end to issue, without weekend filling; it excludes intraday excursions and changes H to recorded nonempty daily observations (`protocol/revision-v8/wp4-daily-fx-design.md:10–28`). The transferred Treasury rule is observation-index geometry, not its calendar cutoff (`:30–49`). The design itself says physical horizons, histories and response change jointly (`:73–90`). Actual data contain 930 daily rows and 501 evaluation issues, not the planning estimate of about 780 (`notes/revision-v8/wp4-acceptance.json:13–23`; `notes/revision-v8/wp4-mismatches.json:8–12`). Historical F wins 7/8 contrasts, but QR4 W260/H6 lowers loss by 2.974% with 37 versus 30 breaches (`results/revision-v8/wp4/COMPLETE_RESULTS.md:13–18`); its classification remains TREASURY_LIKE under the frozen 3/4 rule (`results/revision-v8/wp4/classification.json:60–68,92–128`).

   **Why it matters.** Same-dealer aggregation removes a change of dealer identity in this contrast, but does not hold fixed physical horizon, physical history, feature scales, sampling target or evaluation span. “The frequency effect has been separated” would overstate identification. “Conditional gains require hourly data” would also suppress a completed daily counterexample. The new daily target is not an intraday-peak risk assessment.

   **Feasible response and residual limit.** Add a daily row to the decision map and state both the 7/8 historical wins and the lone QR4 gain with its additional breaches. Call the result consistent with an aggregation/history-scale explanation within one source, rather than a causal decomposition. Retain the 930/501 actual counts, day-end information clock and sampled-target definition in the Supplement. This resolves ambiguous measurement and suppressed-counterexample risks. Pure frequency identification and independent dealer replication remain unsupported; the cover-letter request to claim a separated frequency effect must be narrowed accordingly.

9. **MINOR — Preserve the distinction between true-quantile coverage bounds, fitted-report breach diagnostics and score superiority.**

   **Evidence.** The mathematical claim is a strict upper-tail bound for a true conditional lower inverse quantile, and the endpoint-to-peak bound separately requires independent symmetric increments and a population endpoint quantile (`submission-jof/manuscript.tex:97–111`; `supplement.tex:41–57,76–79`). The main diagnostics correctly identify equality-to-nominal coverage tests as distinct from that inequality (`manuscript.tex:278`), and the modified delayed ACI wrapper imports no canonical theorem (`supplement.tex:930–937`). WP5 H6 QR33 improves matched loss by 12.166527% but records 416 versus 282 breaches; H24 QR33 improves matched loss with 470 versus 275 breaches (`results/revision-v8/wp5/FULL_BACKCAST_RESULTS.md:7,11`). The daily QR4 counterexample similarly has more breaches (`results/revision-v8/wp4/COMPLETE_RESULTS.md:13–15`).

   **Why it matters.** A fitted 95th-quantile report, empirical corrected rank or a numerically successful QR fit does not inherit conditional 5% breach control under these dependent paths. An atom can make a valid true upper quantile conservative, so equality-test nonrejection/rejection is a different question. New score gains must not silently become improved calibration or a guaranteed safe limit.

   **Feasible response and residual limit.** Preserve the current mathematical qualifications and carry paired breach counts beside all newly highlighted score gains. Refer to “observed breach behavior” and “score advantage”; avoid unqualified coverage certification or conditional calibration. Do not add new hit tests or a new ACI comparison. The current theory wording already handles the logical distinction; careful integration can retain it. Conditional coverage, economic decision costs and future operating safety cannot be established from the existing score tables or theorem assumptions.
