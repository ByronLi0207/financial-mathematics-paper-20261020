## C1 — MINOR: Put the inventory-use boundary beside the target and its known floor

**Location and evidence.** `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:83–101` defines the sampled absolute-position maximum, calls a floor-covering report a “feasible peak limit,” and gives the floor-projection result. The important qualifications about aggregate offsets, unobserved excursions and settlement cash are already present at `submission-jof/manuscript.tex:322`; `submission-jof/supplement.tex:41–57` also correctly separates floor projection from true-quantile coverage. The remaining issue is the distance between the positive target definition and its practical-use qualification.

**Operational implication.** A manager reading the target section alone could interpret the known floor as a cash-funding requirement or infer that a statistically coherent report is an approved trading limit. Covering the currently recorded net inventory is an algebraic property of this response; it does not measure settlement obligations, gross positions hidden by netting, between-record peaks, or the consequence of acting on a limit.

**Feasible response.** Add one short use statement after the target definition: “This is a report for monitoring sampled net inventory; covering the known floor does not measure settlement cash needs or establish an economically optimal operating limit.” Retain the fuller existing limitations and the population-versus-fitted distinction. This makes the intended inventory-monitoring use explicit without weakening the valid proposition or inventing a cash model.
