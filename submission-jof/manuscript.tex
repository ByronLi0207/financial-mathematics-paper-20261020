\begin{center}\Large\bfseries Forecasting Peak Net Positions: Known Floors, Endpoint-to-Peak Bounds and Score--Coverage Disagreement\end{center}
Retrospective forecasting evaluation and model validation

\begin{abstract}
Endpoint forecasts do not determine the maximum position reached along a financial path. We study the upper quantile of a sampled absolute net-position peak with a known current-position floor, explicitly matured training labels and matched-history controls. We state a pointwise floor projection, a classical absolute-displacement endpoint-to-peak bound and common-pool construction distances; none creates a new distribution-free coverage guarantee for fitted reports. Three findings delimit the empirical value. The original FX primary lowers mean loss by 12.660\% on the previously inspected record. Its separately identified matched-history short-horizon regression lowers mean pinball loss by 14.77\% but increases breaches from 360 to 376. Elementary-score curves for the distinct original FX primary cross, ruling out dominance for that pair. Horizon-specific short-history FX regression reports cross on 640/6625 origins despite ordered realized targets. The separately fitted Treasury regression primary raises loss by 50.79\%, and the additional aggregate panel yields only 3/48 lower-loss original-regression cells. Exact gain decomposition, label-timing controls and additional mature benchmarks are post-result / exploratory, with mixed directions, failed fits and incomplete upper-target evidence retained. The Treasury exercise replays a downloaded vintage by economic date; compatible unseen-dealer and prospective public-release validation remain outstanding. The evidence supports joint validation of accuracy, calibration, report coherence and measurement compatibility, rather than a universal forecasting or funding advantage.
\end{abstract}
Keywords: quantile forecasting; sampled path maxima; net positions; forecast evaluation; calibration; model validation

\section*{1. Introduction}
Dealers absorb customer order imbalances while managing inventory risk and scarce balance-sheet capacity. Inventory affects optimal dealer pricing (Ho and Stoll, 1981), FX dealing (Lyons, 1995), and observed inventory adjustment in dealership markets (Hansch, Naik and Viswanathan, 1998). Treasury intermediation likewise depends on dealer capacity (Duffie et al., 2023). A forecast of the maximum recorded position magnitude over a future horizon may support monitoring of the measured position: an endpoint can return toward its starting value after an intervening large position. Translating that monitoring signal into a particular margin or financing decision requires the corresponding contractual and balance-sheet inputs. The inventory literature motivates monitoring; this passive forecast comparison identifies no change in optimal quotes, hedges or realized funding requirements.

A position can finish below a monitoring report after exceeding it within the horizon. For the Treasury-bill aggregate at economic date 2021-01-27, a clean frozen-F report of 73,139 USD millions exceeds the absolute endpoint 48,144, yet the sampled path reaches 74,397 (Figure 1). A script selects the earliest original evaluation origin satisfying absolute endpoint \(\le\) clean report \( < \) sampled peak. This post-result / exploratory illustration uses a downloaded vintage and an explicit selection rule; it is not evidence of typicality, a public-release backtest or the unobserved maximum between source records.

\section*{4. Target and forecasting methods}
A forecast origin is the record at which the report is issued. Mature training outcomes are completed future-path labels available by the latest scheduled refit. Fixed-at-refit (frozen) F holds that label pool between refits; per-origin (rolling) F refreshes it at each forecast origin. Both update the observed current anchor. Historical peaks use their original recorded positions; translated increments shift historical signed paths to the current position. Headroom is the nonnegative excess above the observed floor, the absolute current position. These are forecasting definitions, not cash-resource allocations.

\subsection*{Proposition 1. Known-floor projection and strict coverage}
Let the forecast-origin information include B, let Y\(\ge\)B almost surely, and let \(0<\tau<1\). Project any issued report c to max(B,c). Its pinball loss is no larger at every possible Y. A true conditional lower inverse quantile q at level \(\tau=1\)\(-\)\(\alpha\) has strict breach probability at most \(\alpha\). Equality is not automatic: an atom at the quantile can make the probability smaller. The lower bound alone need not create an outcome atom; fitted reports do not inherit the population statement.

\[
\rho_\tau(Y-c)-\rho_\tau(Y-\max\{B,c\})=\tau(B-c)_+,\qquad \Pr(Y>q\mid\mathcal F_t)\le\alpha.
\]
Proof. Below B both errors are nonnegative, so their loss difference is the displayed nonnegative quantity; otherwise the report is unchanged. For the conditional inverse quantile, F(q)\(\ge\)\(\tau\), hence the strict-tail probability is at most \(\alpha\). An atom is a possible reason for strict inequality, not a necessary premise. Accordingly, the retained equality-based coverage tests are diagnostics of their stated nulls, not automatic tests of every valid upper quantile.

\subsection*{Proposition 2. Classical endpoint-to-peak bound}
Let future real increments be independent and individually symmetric about zero. Write S\textsubscript{h} for their partial sums, S\textsubscript{0}=0, and fix an observed starting position x with \(B=|x|\). No identical-distribution or finite-moment assumption is required. If q is the population (1\(-\)\(\alpha\)/2) quantile of |S\textsubscript{H}|, the report B+q has strict peak-breach probability at most \(\alpha\). This is a direct scalar application of the classical Lévy maximal inequality (Sussman, 2023), not a new inequality.

\[
\Pr\!\left(\max_{0\le h\le H}|x+S_h|>B+q\right)\le\Pr\!\left(\max_h|S_h|>q\right)\le2\Pr(|S_H|>q)\le\alpha.
\]
Proof. The triangle inequality gives the first inclusion; Lévy's maximal inequality and the inverse-quantile tail bound give the rest. For conditional coverage, independence and symmetry must hold conditional on origin information and q must be the corresponding conditional population quantile. The empirical plug-in L, overlapping historical labels and dependent mean-reverting data do not acquire that guarantee. A generic one-sided endpoint VaR is not the required absolute-displacement quantile. Supplement S15 gives an exact finite counterexample to that substitution; Supplement S11 retains the proof, original simulations and corrected sensitivities.

\subsection*{Proposition 3. Common-pool construction distances}
Use one nonempty historical pool, one available order-statistic rank k, and peaks that include their starting observation. Let F add historical peak headroom to the current B, S translate each historical signed path to x, and K floor the historical-peak order statistic at B. Their differences obey the following deterministic bounds. The common pool and same rank are essential; these are neither forecast-accuracy nor coverage guarantees.

\[
|F-K|\le r_{\rm abs}=\max_i\bigl||N_i|-B\bigr|,\qquad |S-K|\le r_{\rm signed}=\max_i|N_i-x|.
\]
Proof. For each historical row, compare the headroom construction and translated path maximum to max(B,P\textsubscript{i}). Projection and the reverse triangle inequality bound their differences by the two anchor distances. A fixed order statistic is nonexpansive for the maximum paired difference, and flooring commutes with that order statistic. Applying these facts yields the displayed bounds. Supplement S2 retains the rowwise derivation and examples where the same observed history permits opposite loss rankings.

The operational report L uses the same frozen completed-origin pool and refit times as F. Its endpoint sample is \(|N_{i+H}-N_i|\) and its one-based order rank is \(\lceil39(W+1)/40\rceil\). Add the resulting estimate to \(B_t\). This empirical plug-in is distinct from the known population quantile in Proposition 2. Overlapping endpoint samples and dependent, changing series do not inherit the proposition's population coverage. Conditional coverage additionally requires the independence and symmetry premises and the correct endpoint quantile conditional on issue information. The bound supplies no pinball-loss ordering against F.

Observed targets satisfy \(P(H6)\le P(H24)\), and population conditional quantiles under the same origin information inherit that ordering. Independently estimated forecasts need not: FX QR W720 crosses on 640/6625 issues (Supplement Table S15). A raise-only repair of the longer-horizon report can increase pinball loss, as the exact counterexample in Supplement S15 shows. This does not rule out all monotone rearrangements: with equally weighted ordered outcomes and a common quantile level, sorting can weakly reduce total pinball loss without guaranteeing each horizon improves. Crossing is a coherence diagnostic, not sufficient proof of calibration failure.

The historical controls also use current information: the headroom and floored-peak rules incorporate the observed magnitude, and path translation uses the current signed anchor. Conditional estimation is therefore compared with already anchored historical forecasts, not with a rule that ignores the current observation.

Forecasts are scored in native source units using the stipulated upper-quantile pinball preference. The paired loss difference is left minus right, so a negative difference favors the left procedure:

\[
e=P-C,\qquad L_{.95}=.95(e)_+ +.05(-e)_+,\qquad D=\frac1M\sum_t\{L(P_t,C_t^{\rm left})-L(P_t,C_t^{\rm right})\}.
\]
The stipulated pinball score weights an underprediction dollar nineteen times an overprediction dollar. It is a consistent quantile score (Gneiting, 2011), rather than an observed financial cost. We report \(U=\operatorname{mean}[(P-C)_+]\), the mean underprediction amount, and \(V=\operatorname{mean}[(C-P)_+]\), the mean overprediction amount. Both average over all forecast origins; conditional breach severity averages over each method's own strict \(P>C\) event set. The reported headroom C\(-\)B is distinct from V. Archived fields mean\_positive\_overrun, mean\_unused\_allowance and mean\_extra\_allowance correspond to U, V and mean(C\(-\)B); their historical names do not represent resources actually allocated. Lower mean loss and fewer breach occasions are separate outcomes. Financial units are never pooled across sources.

After exact trace reproduction, we compare paired loss differences using Diebold--Mariano (1995) statistics with Bartlett Newey--West (1987) covariance. The fixed bandwidth is \(L=\max\{H,\lfloor4(n/100)^{2/9}\rfloor\}\); doubled bandwidth supplies a sensitivity check. Harvey, Leybourne and Newbold (1997) supply the reported small-sample multiplier, here used as an approximation for pinball loss. Fixed finite estimation windows motivate the unconditional predictive-ability interpretation of Giacomini and White (2006), under mixing, adequate high-order moments and positive long-run variance. Shared training can extend dependence beyond the target horizon. These are working assumptions for this post-result analysis.

\section*{9. Implications for model validation}
Jointly report quantile loss, strict-breach frequency and dependence-aware calibration diagnostics. Add elementary-score curves and sensitivity to stipulated loss preferences when an average-score gain might be interpreted as safer limits. A proper quantile score is not systematically biased toward more breaches; it evaluates magnitudes, whereas breach frequency discards them. Equality-based hit tests and valid population upper-quantile inequalities also require distinct interpretations.

Use frequency, history and the measurement definition to frame validation comparisons before selecting a method family. Table 6 summarizes the executed evidence and its cautions; it is a descriptive map, not a new switching algorithm. Retain historical and parsimonious controls alongside fitted procedures, compare matching completed-label pools, and preserve numerical failures and adverse cells. The observed contrasts do not isolate a causal frequency effect.

For endpoint-based peak bounds, first verify that the available input is an absolute cumulative-displacement quantile at the required level. A generic one-sided endpoint VaR is not interchangeable. Check conditional independence and symmetry if conditional coverage is claimed, and distinguish a population quantile from an estimated overlapping-label plug-in. The classical bound can be conservative or lose its guarantee outside its premises; no cost or operational-resource reduction follows automatically.

\section*{10. Interpretation and limitations}
The executed QR specifications perform poorly on the weekly Treasury task. Their 25 level coordinates span the current observation through 24 weeks of history. The two hour coordinates, two weekday coordinates and gap indicator are exactly constant on this weekly source. Training-only centering makes those five columns exactly zero, so they provide no predictive variation and are not a demonstrated mechanism of overfitting. The original nominal design has 33 predictors plus an intercept, or 34 coefficients, with at most 28 varying columns and 29 potentially predictive coefficients before other collinearity, against \(W=52\) overlapping training labels in the shorter history. All four Treasury penalty selections are menu endpoints. Its 52 validation origins contain at most 3 disjoint H24 label paths, a path count rather than an effective-sample-size estimate. Correlated lag levels, overlapping labels, restricted tuning, target dynamics and reporting composition remain competing explanations. Neither the parsimonious sensitivity nor the aggregate panel separates those mechanisms.

The four-predictor sensitivity uses B, signed N and short/long increment RMS, with validation penalties \{0.001,0.01,0.1,1,10\}. It lowers loss against the original QR in 5 of 8 cells and against matched F in 3 cells. In the Treasury primary cell its loss change is -32.2\% relative to the original QR and 2.17\% relative to F. All adverse cells remain in the complete Supplement tables. The exercise changes features and the penalty menu jointly, so it measures a parsimonious specification sensitivity rather than identifying a feature-only cause or rehabilitating the original primary.

The contrast between the sources concerns a separately fitted procedure family: each source receives its own transforms, coefficient estimates, temporal scales and validation selection. The Treasury deterioration is an accuracy failure of the executed QR specifications. The exploratory AR comparison shows that the observed Treasury loss ranking differs across the executed families.

Shared histories, overlapping labels, finite validation and investigator selection limit statistical generalization. An atom at the true quantile may lower its strict-breach probability below the nominal tail probability; the known floor alone does not imply such an atom. Numerical acceptance supplies no conditional-coverage guarantee. Horizon-specific mature scenario sets differ, so zero observed crossings cannot establish population coherence.

Stipulated shortfall/excess weights are not observed financing prices. Quotes, fills, hedges, book decomposition, collateral and cash flows would be required to map this passive envelope into an operational inventory, margin or funding policy. This study evaluates forecasts, not such interventions; Avellaneda and Stoikov (2008) provide a distinct active inventory-sensitive quoting framework.

The executed finite projected delayed ACI-style, headroom SAV and fixed combinations in Supplement S16 broaden the benchmark menu without representing their entire method classes. Canonical sequential conformal procedures, richer mean or volatility dynamics, and innovation-tail conditional EVT remain distinct alternatives. The fixed FHS, QRF and peak-POT additions in Supplement S13 retain their failures and resource limits. Matching mature-label pools and update schedules leaves feature lookbacks, assumptions and estimation rules different. Compatible new dealer observations, historical release vintages and decision-linked inputs remain necessary for broader portability.

A post-result / exploratory selected-fit readback examines the Treasury C2 menu boundary directly (Supplement S10). At \(\lambda=10\), standardized response-normalized slope norms range from 0.001497 to 0.01937. Removing slopes while keeping each fitted intercept changes capacity by a mean absolute 136.8--832.3 source USD millions across the four cells. Relative to the lower empirical headroom-quantile report I, observed loss is 0.2432--2.192\% lower; this descriptive difference has no dedicated selection-adjusted test. The fits have small normalized slopes and high headroom correlation with I, but are not identical to I or F. A boundary penalty and high capacity correlation do not by themselves show zero feature value. Three of the four C2 cells still have higher loss than F.

The expanded aggregate panel strengthens the negative transport finding but does not isolate its mechanism. Features and tuning menus, finite sample size, target behavior and reporting-regime changes can all matter. Downloaded cross-era histories are not original-release vintages; metadata/provenance for each key are retained. A prospective public-release protocol freezes the four procedures and selected historical-validation penalties before future issue artifacts. The first planned new release is after the protocol freeze, rather than retroactively labeling the already published October 14 observation as unseen. Because the release clock lags economic dates, the target includes prediction of unpublished measurements, with a nowcast/forecast mixture. A dated local amendment fixes future path values at their first actual capture and logs later revisions separately; operational capture and missing-week handling still require a bound adapter (Supplement S12). Results await prior timestamped report issuance and target publication; no live prospective validation is claimed.

Anticipated concerns. The Kaggle source supplies a single attributed position series, not independently audited dealer books; original-source documentation, fingerprints and explicit clock checks partly support reproducibility but cannot verify commercial completeness or measurement accuracy. Dealer aggregation permits cancellation and valuation or composition changes; source-unit definitions and the full related-task panel preserve that measured interpretation rather than treating it as individual exposure. The FX interval was previously inspected, so later tests and model choices remain retrospective; preserved primaries, complete adverse outcomes and time-respecting labels reduce selective reporting but do not create a fresh holdout.

\section*{11. Conclusion}
\textbf{RQ1 --- Accuracy.} Conditional regression improves the inspected FX short-horizon score, while the separately fitted Treasury regression primary deteriorates. The exact additive bridge separates the joint history/update contrast from the conditional-procedure contrast, without identifying causal estimator effects. Historical, neighbor and conditional-model rankings depend on the executed comparison.

\textbf{RQ2 --- Evaluation.} A score gain can coexist with more breach occasions, crossed elementary-score curves and horizon-incoherent fitted reports. The floor projection and classical construction properties clarify what can be guaranteed pointwise or under population assumptions; the fitted empirical reports have no automatic coverage guarantee. Score, calibration and report-coherence evidence must retain their distinct nulls and meanings.

\textbf{RQ3 --- Transport.} Parsimonious regression, related aggregate tasks and strong implemented baselines give mixed and frequently adverse outcomes. The observed aggregate failures delimit portability across financial measurement processes; they are not independent dealer replications or proof that all conditional methods fail.

The evidential next steps are execution of the timestamped prospective public-release protocol, access to a compatible unseen dealer series, and evaluation of the remaining method classes under explicit mature-label timing. The additional finite adaptations and combinations do not close those evidence gaps. Investigator selection, downloaded vintages, unexecuted alternatives and the missing decision-cost mapping constrain interpretation. All original adverse cells and unsuccessful fits remain in the Supplement.
