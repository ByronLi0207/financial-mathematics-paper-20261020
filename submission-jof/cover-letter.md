Dear Editors,

Please consider “When Is History Long Enough? Conditional Quantile Models versus Historical Rules for Peak Net-Position Limits” for Journal of Forecasting.

This paper asks when conditional forecasts of sampled absolute-position peaks improve on historical rules using matched information. It compares a market-maker FX record with related weekly dealer-position aggregates, preserving score–breach disagreements, reference-sensitive rankings and adverse conditional results. The target differs from the closest signed-endpoint interval task, while its known-floor argument is elementary and its endpoint-to-peak inequality is classical.

The revision treats history relative to horizon as a post hoc organizing coordinate and records subsequent simulation and financial predictions before their new calculations. New innovation laws satisfy the specified short-history and long-history loss-sign screens, but the universal simplification prediction fails. The financial learning curves reuse a familiar period: identified crossings are consistent with broad predicted bands, while left censoring prevents complete confirmation and long-history reversals rule out a permanent threshold. Shared simulation streams, conditional bootstrap envelopes, differing procedure menus and a changing historical reference prevent a unique estimation-cost interpretation.

The earlier controlled simulation still fails its narrow-regime explanation. Same-dealer daily aggregation does not isolate frequency, and the expanded backcast overlaps previously scored targets with later-selected penalties. These adverse findings remain part of the comparative application. The original primary comparisons and scientific archives are unchanged.

The contribution is bounded forecasting and model-validation evidence for a precisely defined path-dependent position target, using mature methods and completed labels. It is neither a new quantile estimator nor a general causal mechanism, an operating limit policy or independent dealer validation. Actual decision costs, prospective performance and operational continuity remain unmeasured or unevaluated.

The materials include the main manuscript, preserved Online Supplement and technical material, a diagnostic addendum correcting evidence-display completeness, the title-page template and public code/result repository. The author must personally complete and confirm authorship, permissions and all journal-required declarations before any submission.
